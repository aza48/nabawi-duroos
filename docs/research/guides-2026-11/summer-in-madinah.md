<p>Checked 9 November 2026. Summer in Madinah is long and very hot: June, July and August see daytime highs of 42 to 45°C, with the worst days higher, and nights that stay near 30°C. It is also the University's long break, when most scholarship students fly home on their yearly ticket and the city empties. This page covers the heat, the money and the break.</p>

<h2>How hot, and when</h2>
<table><thead><tr><th>Month</th><th>Typical daytime high</th><th>Typical night low</th><th>What it means</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>May</td><td>38 to 40°C</td><td>25°C</td><td>Hot. Outdoor things before 9 am and after Maghrib</td></tr>
<tr><td>June to August</td><td>42 to 45°C</td><td>28 to 30°C</td><td>Very hot. The courtyards of the Haram are hot to the touch by mid-morning. Dust storms come some afternoons</td></tr>
<tr><td>September</td><td>40 to 42°C</td><td>26°C</td><td>Still very hot until the last week</td></tr>
<tr><td>October</td><td>33 to 36°C</td><td>21°C</td><td>The heat breaks. Evenings are pleasant again</td></tr>
</tbody></table>

<h2>Staying safe in the heat</h2>
<ul>
<li>Stay out of direct sun between 10 am and 4 pm. Walk on the shaded side, use the umbrellas of the Haram courtyards, and carry an umbrella of your own; light colours reflect heat.</li>
<li>Drink before you are thirsty. Two to four litres a day in summer is normal. Water coolers stand at every gate of the Haram and in most masjids.</li>
<li>Headache, dizziness, heavy sweating, nausea and cramps mean heat exhaustion: get into air conditioning, drink, cool your neck and wrists with water, and rest. Confusion, collapse or hot dry skin is heatstroke: call 997 at once and cool the person with water while you wait.</li>
<li>Children, pregnant women, older parents and anyone fasting on a hot day are most at risk. Fasting the six days of Shawwal or Mondays and Thursdays in summer means a very long day; plan a quiet afternoon.</li>
<li>Cars become ovens. Never leave a child or an animal in one, and let the air conditioning run before you get in.</li>
<li>The Ministry of Health's advice line is 937, 24 hours, in several languages.</li>
</ul>
<blockquote><p>Tip: Fajr and the hour after it is the best time of a summer day in Madinah. Students who shift their day to pray Fajr in the Haram, study until Dhuhr, sleep in the afternoon and go out after Maghrib find summer easy.</p></blockquote>

<h2>What summer does to your bills</h2>
<p>Air conditioning is most of a summer electricity bill. The residential tariff in 2026 is 18 halalas per kilowatt-hour up to 6,000 kWh a month and 30 halalas above that, plus 15% VAT. From 1 January 2027 it rises to 21 halalas for the first 6,000 kWh and 33 above, and a fixed monthly charge of about 70 SAR for a normal household breaker is added. A two-bedroom flat with two split units running most of the day uses 1,000 to 2,000 kWh in a summer month, so expect 200 to 500 SAR a month in summer against 80 to 150 in winter, and more under the 2027 tariff. Set the thermostat to 24°C, service the filters in May, and close the curtains on the sunny side.</p>

<h2>Before you fly: exit and re-entry</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Students with an iqama need an exit and re-entry visa issued by the University through Travel Management: 200 SAR for a single visa of up to two months, 100 SAR for each extra month, 500 SAR for a multiple visa of three months. Ask for it in good time and check it in Absher or Muqeem before you book.</li>
<li>The visa cannot run past your iqama's expiry, and if the iqama expires while you are abroad the visa dies with it. If your iqama expires in the summer, have it renewed first.</li>
<li>Come back before the visa's return date. Extending from abroad is possible on Absher only while the visa is still valid, at double the price.</li>
<li>Visitor ID holders: your Visitor ID is tied to your visa. Check in Absher that the visa is valid for your return date, and ask Travel Management if it expires during the break.</li>
<li>Pay any traffic fines first. A fine blocks the visa.</li>
</ol>
<p>Fees and the fine print are in <a href="/guides/iqama-and-exit-reentry-fees">Iqama, exit and re-entry, and dependent fees</a>.</p>

<h2>If you stay: summer courses and quiet study</h2>
<ul>
<li>The Prophet's Mosque runs a summer course in Qur'an and mutun each year in Muharram and Safar, in person and online, for men and women, with two daily sessions (after Fajr and after Asr). In 1448 it ran from 5 to 31 July 2026. Registration is through the Haram's educational system.</li>
<li>Several of the mashayikh pause their regular duroos over the summer and the Hajj season and restart in Rabi' al-Awwal; the Duroos page shows which lessons are paused.</li>
<li>Intensive dawrat run in other cities (Qassim, Riyadh, Jazan) in Safar, some with housing.</li>
<li>The University's central library is open through the summer semester, Sunday to Thursday, and is air-conditioned and quiet.</li>
<li>Madinah's own residents go out after Isha: the parks, the Quba walkway and the date farms are busy until well after midnight in summer.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Families in summer</h2>
<ul>
<li>Schools close from late June to late August. Soft-play centres and mall crèches are the daytime answer; parks after Maghrib. See <a href="/guides/kids-activities">Play areas and fun places for children</a>.</li>
<li>Pools: compounds and some hotels sell summer passes; the University sports centre has a pool for men.</li>
<li>The date harvest begins in late July and the date market is at its best in August and September. See <a href="/guides/dates-of-madinah">Dates of Madinah</a>.</li>
</ul>
