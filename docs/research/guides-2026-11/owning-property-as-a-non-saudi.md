# NEW GUIDE: Buying or renting long term: what non-Saudis can and cannot own in Madinah

Proposed slug: `owning-property-as-a-non-saudi`
Stage: `getting-settled` (or `family`)
Title: Buying a home in Madinah as a non-Saudi: what the law allows
Summary: The updated Law of Real Estate Ownership by Non-Saudis, in force since 22 January 2026, lets residents and non-residents buy inside approved zones, with special rules for Makkah and Madinah. What you need, how the application runs on the Saudi Properties portal, what it costs, and when renting is still the better choice.

Source: the Real Estate General Authority's own guide, "دليل تملك غير السعوديين للعقار وفق اللائحة التنفيذية والنطاقات الجغرافية", updated 23 June 2026 (docs/research/raw/2026-11-09/rega-non-saudi-property-ownership-ar-2026-06-23.pdf), read in Arabic, 20 pages. The French translation beside it was not used as a source.

## What is missing today

- MESH has nothing on owning property. `visas-for-family-and-visitors` mentions Premium Residency and property only as a route to residency.
- Families who have been in Madinah for years, and parents who want a base near the Haram, ask whether they can buy. Until 2026 the answer for Madinah was no for almost everyone. It has changed, with conditions.

## What the REGA guide says (extracted for the content agent)

Pages 3 and 4: The updated Law was approved by Royal Decree M/14 of 19 Muharram 1447 (14 July 2025). It has 15 articles, an implementing regulation of 14 articles, and approved geographic zones. The regulation covers registration and identity checks, disclosure of owners for non-Saudi companies, applications through the electronic portal, electronic payment, special cases, and the fees due on some transactions and their exemptions.

Page 7: Six categories: (1) resident individuals, (2) Premium Residency holders, (3) individuals outside the Kingdom, (4) GCC citizens, (5) non-Saudi companies and non-profit entities, (6) other legal persons decided case by case.

Page 8: Makkah and Madinah have a special status. Ownership there is limited to Muslims only and within a limited zone. Riyadh and Jeddah have their own limited zones.

Page 10: Applications and all financial transactions go through the Saudi Properties portal (saudiproperties.rega.gov.sa) and electronic payment under the Saudi Central Bank's payment systems; the procedure ends in the Real Estate Register (RER, السجل العقاري) with the deed. Five steps: prepare requirements (valid iqama or Premium Residency, or a digital ID for non-residents; a bank account; a Saudi mobile number), choose the property and check it is inside an approved zone, log in to the portal, verify eligibility and obtain the eligibility certificate (شهادة الأهلية), complete the purchase, pay fees and taxes, and transfer ownership electronically.

Page 11 matrix for individuals:
- Resident (iqama holder): inside the zone, all cities, yes; Makkah and Madinah, yes on condition of being Muslim. Outside the zone: one property for own residence in any city except Makkah and Madinah; Makkah and Madinah, no.
- Premium Residency holder: inside the zone, yes everywhere, Makkah and Madinah on condition of Islam. Outside the zone: residential, commercial and industrial property in all cities except Makkah and Madinah under the Premium Residency law; in Makkah and Madinah a usufruct right (حق الانتفاع) under the Premium Residency law.
- Non-resident individual: inside the zone yes, Makkah and Madinah on condition of Islam; outside the zone, no.
- GCC citizen: inside the zone yes, Makkah and Madinah on condition of Islam; outside the zone in all cities except Makkah and Madinah under the GCC ownership rules.
- Non-Saudi companies, non-profits and other legal persons: inside the zone in all cities except Makkah and Madinah; nothing in Makkah and Madinah, nothing outside the zone.
- Article 5 note: nothing in the Law reduces the better rights that the Premium Residency law, the GCC ownership rules or other laws already give.

Page 12: General requirements for a non-resident natural person: obtain the digital ID, open a bank account in the Kingdom in his name, issue a Saudi mobile number in his name linked to the digital ID. Spouse and descendants rule: the spouse and descendants of a non-Saudi are treated as his dependants for residential property under article 2(3); none of them may own a residential property on their own unless the marriage has ended or the descendant has reached 25.

Pages 13 to 14: Resident: must hold a valid iqama or Premium Residency, disclose all data on the transaction, and the property must be registered in the Real Estate Register. Ownership gives the rights of an owner in rem under the law. Non-residents can request a digital ID from a Saudi mission abroad.

Page 18: REGA monitors compliance. Penalties take account of the nature and effect of the breach; a correction period of 10 to 180 days is set in the decision. Notices sent through the portal or by SMS to the phone linked to government platforms count as served.

Page 19: Support: 920017183 inside the Kingdom, 00966920017183 from abroad, saudiproperties@rega.gov.sa, portal saudiproperties.rega.gov.sa. The zones map is on the portal.

## READY-TO-PASTE DRAFT

```html
<p>Checked 9 November 2026. Since 22 January 2026 non-Saudis can own property in Saudi Arabia under the updated Law of Real Estate Ownership by Non-Saudis. Madinah is covered, with two conditions that apply nowhere else except Makkah: the buyer must be Muslim, and the property must lie inside the approved zone for the city. This page explains who qualifies, what the application looks like and what it costs, and when renting remains the sensible choice.</p>

<h2>Who can buy in Madinah</h2>
<table><thead><tr><th>You are</th><th>Inside the approved zone in Madinah</th><th>Outside the zone in Madinah</th><th>Elsewhere in the Kingdom</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>A resident with a valid iqama</td><td>Yes, if you are Muslim</td><td>No</td><td>Inside zones: yes. Outside zones: one home for your own use in any city except Makkah and Madinah</td></tr>
<tr><td>A Premium Residency holder</td><td>Yes, if you are Muslim</td><td>A usufruct right (long lease registered as a right) under the Premium Residency law, not ownership</td><td>Residential, commercial and industrial property in all cities except Makkah and Madinah</td></tr>
<tr><td>Living outside the Kingdom</td><td>Yes, if you are Muslim, with a digital ID from a Saudi embassy or consulate</td><td>No</td><td>Inside zones only</td></tr>
<tr><td>A GCC citizen</td><td>Yes, if you are Muslim</td><td>No</td><td>Under the GCC ownership rules, all cities except Makkah and Madinah</td></tr>
<tr><td>A foreign company or non-profit</td><td>No</td><td>No</td><td>Inside zones only</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>Students on a Visitor ID are not residents under this law, so they fall under the "living outside the Kingdom" column: ownership inside the zone is possible with a digital ID, a Saudi bank account and a Saudi mobile number in your name. Since a Visitor ID holder already has an Absher digital identity, a bank account and a Saudi number, the requirements are met in practice, and the portal decides the eligibility certificate.</p>
<blockquote><p>Note: The zone map for Madinah is published on the Saudi Properties portal. Check the exact plot there before you negotiate anything, because a property one street outside the line cannot be bought by a non-Saudi.</p></blockquote>

<h2>Spouse and children</h2>
<p>For residential property, your spouse and your children count as one household with you. None of them can own a home on their own under this law unless the marriage has ended or the child has reached 25. A family buys one home in one name.</p>

<h2>What you need before you apply</h2>
<ul>
<li>A valid iqama or Premium Residency, or, for non-residents, the digital ID for non-Saudis.</li>
<li>A bank account inside the Kingdom in your own name.</li>
<li>A Saudi mobile number in your name, linked to your digital identity.</li>
<li>The property must already be registered in the Real Estate Register (السجل العقاري) with an electronic deed. A property with an old paper deed has to be registered first by its owner.</li>
<li>Full disclosure of every detail of the purchase, including where the money comes from. Payment is electronic only, through the Saudi Central Bank's payment systems. No cash.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The application, step by step</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Sign in to the Saudi Properties portal (saudiproperties.rega.gov.sa) with Nafath, or with the non-resident digital ID.</li>
<li>Open the zone map and confirm the property sits inside Madinah's approved zone.</li>
<li>Run the eligibility check in the portal. It issues an eligibility certificate (شهادة الأهلية) that names you and your category.</li>
<li>Agree the price with the seller and sign through a licensed real-estate broker or notary, with the deed number from the Real Estate Register.</li>
<li>Pay the price and the fees electronically. The real estate transaction tax of 5% is paid on every sale in the Kingdom, and the Law lets the Authority add a fee on transactions by non-Saudis of up to 5% of the value. The portal shows the exact amounts before you pay.</li>
<li>The transfer is completed electronically in the Real Estate Register and your deed is issued in your name.</li>
</ol>
<p>Breaches of the Law carry penalties set by a committee, with a correction period of 10 to 180 days. Notices arrive through the portal or by SMS to the number linked to your digital identity, and they count as delivered.</p>

<h2>Buying or renting: a plain comparison</h2>
<table><thead><tr><th>Question</th><th>Rent</th><th>Buy</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Up-front money</td><td>Six or twelve months' rent plus a deposit</td><td>The full price (mortgages for non-Saudis depend on each bank's rules), plus 5% transaction tax, plus any non-Saudi fee of up to 5%, plus broker and registration fees</td></tr>
<tr><td>How long you will stay</td><td>Any length. Contracts run a year and renew</td><td>Worth it only for many years. Selling again means finding a buyer who is allowed to buy the same property</td></tr>
<tr><td>Your status changes</td><td>Leave at the end of the contract</td><td>You keep the property if you leave the Kingdom; non-residents can own inside the zone</td></tr>
<tr><td>Where</td><td>Anywhere in the city</td><td>Inside the approved zone only</td></tr>
<tr><td>Paperwork</td><td>Ejar contract, 125 SAR a year</td><td>Eligibility certificate, Real Estate Register deed, disclosure</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>For students and most families, renting through Ejar remains the right choice: see <a href="/guides/finding-an-apartment">Finding and renting a flat</a>. Buying suits families settled in the city for the long term and people abroad who want a home for their visits.</p>
```
