# NEW GUIDE: Leaving Madinah for good

Proposed slug: `leaving-madinah-for-good`
Stage: `leaving`
Title: Leaving Madinah for good: the full checklist
Summary: Everything that has to happen before your final flight, in order: your University clearance and final exit, the last stipend and the graduation allowance, your documents and their legalisation, closing your bank account and SIM, ending your lease, selling or giving away your things, shipping books home, your ticket and baggage, and what to keep for later.

Research drafted 9 November 2026. WebFetch to Saudi government hosts (absher.sa, my.gov.sa, splonline.com.sa, rega.gov.sa, ejar.sa, rulebook.sama.gov.sa, spa.gov.sa) was blocked in this session, so official pages were read through search excerpts. Every official URL is listed so the content agent or admins can open it.

## Why this guide

`graduate-procedures` covers the University side well (room handover, card, degree, stamp, MOFA, embassy, bisht, ticket). It says nothing about the life outside the campus that also has to end: the bank account, the SIM and Absher, the flat and its deposit, the furniture, the books, the baggage, the airport, and what to keep. The group chat asked three times in summer 2026 what happens to bank accounts, SIMs and tickets at final exit and got no answer (docs/research/group-chat-2026.md rows 83 to 85, 241). This guide is the answer, and it links to `graduate-procedures` for the campus steps rather than repeating them.

## What is missing today (across all guides)

- No single timeline that puts the final exit visa, the University clearance, the flat, the bank and the flight in the right order.
- Nothing on the final exit visa's own rules: 60 days to leave, the iqama must have 30 days left, fines block it.
- Nothing for Visitor ID students on how leaving differs (no iqama to hand in, nothing to cancel at Jawazat).
- Nothing on closing or freezing a bank account, or how the Saudi Central Bank's rules treat an account after the iqama or Visitor ID ends.
- Nothing on cancelling SIMs, or what happens to a number after final departure.
- Nothing on ending an Ejar lease, the move-out form and getting the deposit back.
- Nothing on selling furniture and where students sell it.
- Nothing on shipping books: carriers, weight limits, volumetric weight, how to pack, customs at home.
- Nothing on airline baggage and excess fees, the airport bus and the time to allow.
- Nothing on the apostille route for documents going to Hague countries, which replaces embassy legalisation for many students.
- Nothing on what to download and keep before University accounts close.

Some steps have deadlines set by Jawazat, some by the University and some by your landlord, and several of them depend on each other. Work through this page from the top, tick things off as you go, and start at least eight weeks before the day you want to fly.</p>

<h2>The order of things</h2>

<tr><td>The day</td><td>Passport, final exit printout or Visitor ID, ticket, documents in hand luggage. Be at the airport three hours before an international flight.</td></tr>

<h2>1. Your residency: final exit or Visitor ID</h2>
<p>How you leave depends on which document you hold.</p>
<table><thead><tr><th>Topic</th><th>Student iqama</th><th>Visitor ID (educational visa)</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>What you need to leave for good</td><td>A final exit visa (خروج نهائي), issued by your sponsor, the University, through Travel Management</td><td>No final exit visa. There is no iqama to cancel. Ask Travel Management through Tawasul what clearance it needs from you before you book</td></tr>
<tr><td>How long it is valid</td><td>60 days from the day it is issued. Leave within them</td><td>Your Visitor ID is tied to your visa and ends when you leave for good</td></tr>
<tr><td>Before it can be issued</td><td>Your iqama must have at least 30 days left. Traffic fines, unpaid government fees and any file open against you block it</td><td>Pay any traffic fines and settle anything open on Absher</td></tr>
<tr><td>Fee</td><td>Free</td><td>Nothing to pay</td></tr>
<tr><td>Changing your mind</td><td>A final exit visa can be cancelled before you leave, through the sponsor. Once you have left on it, your iqama is finished</td><td>Leave normally</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<blockquote><p>Tip: Pay traffic fines before you ask for the final exit. Fines appear in Absher under Violations, and a fine recorded after the visa is issued can still stop you at the airport.</p></blockquote>
<p>Ask for the final exit only when the rest of your clearance is done and your flight is close. The 60 days start on the day of issue, not the day you choose to fly.</p>

<h2>2. Your University clearance, money and ticket</h2>
<p>The campus steps are in <a href="/guides/graduate-procedures">Graduating: room handover, certificates, legalisation and your flight home</a>. In short: check your name, download your documents, hand back the room with its five items of furniture, return your card, collect your degree, get the University stamp, legalise, then the ticket. Three things about money:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Your last stipend.</strong> The stipend is paid into the IBAN on your file. Keep that account open until the last payment has landed.</li>
<li><strong>The graduation allowance.</strong> Full scholarship students receive three months' stipend for shipping their books home once the University has confirmed their clearance. It goes to the same IBAN, so do not close the account before it arrives.</li>
<li><strong>The ticket.</strong> Your final ticket is issued against a travel order on Etimad, to a capital city in your country or the country that issued your visa. It is valid for three months from the first travel date, one cabin bag and two checked bags, no cash refund, and a no-show loses it. The rules are in <a href="/guides/etimad-account">Setting up your Etimad account</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. Your documents</h2>
<p>Download everything from the Academic System while your login still works: the transcript, the graduation certificate and the conduct certificate. Save PDFs to cloud storage and to a second device. Then legalise.</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Get the University stamp on copies of your degree, transcript, graduation certificate and conduct certificate.</li>
<li>Open <a href="https://ratification.mofa.gov.sa">ratification.mofa.gov.sa</a>, sign in with Nafath, add each document and pay the fee per document. Book the Madinah branch and go with the receipts and the stamped copies.</li>
<li>Decide between an apostille and embassy legalisation. Saudi Arabia joined the Hague Apostille Convention on 7 December 2022. If your country is also a member, an apostille from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs is all your documents need, and your embassy has no part to play. If your country is not a member, take the Ministry's legalisation to your embassy in Riyadh or consulate in Jeddah as well.</li>
<li>Saudi Post offers a service that carries documents to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs for legalisation and brings them back, so you do not have to attend the branch. Ask at a Saudi Post branch or on 19992.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote><p>Tip: Check the member list of the Hague Convention for your country before you book anything at the embassy. Most of Europe, the UK, the US, Australia, India, Pakistan (since 2023), Indonesia, Malaysia and South Africa are members. Many African countries are not.</p></blockquote>

<h2>4. Your bank account</h2>
<p>The Saudi Central Bank's rules tell banks to freeze all accounts of a non-Saudi when the residence permit expires, and the same applies to a Visitor ID account when the Visitor ID runs out. A frozen account can still receive money but cannot pay out. To withdraw the balance or close the account while you are still in the Kingdom, the bank asks to see your final exit visa or a letter from Jawazat. So the order is:</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Wait for the last stipend and the graduation allowance to land.</li>
<li>Transfer the balance home through your bank's international transfer or a licensed remittance app, in riyals or in your home currency, while your iqama or Visitor ID is still valid. See <a href="/guides/sending-and-receiving-money">Sending money home and receiving money</a>.</li>
<li>Pay off any card balance or instalment plan (Tabby, Tamara and similar) and cancel standing payments.</li>
<li>Ask the bank in writing to close the account, and keep the closure confirmation. If you leave it open it will be frozen, and getting the balance out from abroad means notarised letters and months of waiting.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote><p>Tip: Keep a screenshot of your IBAN and your account statement for the last year. Some graduates need them later for a scholarship, a visa or a tax return at home.</p></blockquote>

<h2>5. Your SIM cards and Absher</h2>
<ul>
<li>Check how many SIMs are registered to you on Mutasil (sign in with Nafath). Cancel every line you will not keep, at the operator's shop or in its app, and pay any postpaid balance. An unpaid postpaid bill can turn into a claim against you.</li>
<li>Operators treat your final departure from the Kingdom as grounds to cancel a line, and a cancelled number loses its balance and can be given to someone else. Do not rely on a Saudi number staying alive after you leave.</li>
<li>Absher sends its codes to your registered Saudi number. Finish everything you need Absher for (fines, the final exit printout, your Visitor ID record) before you cancel the SIM that is linked to it.</li>
<li>Download your Visitor ID or iqama details, your final exit visa and your Absher violation record as PDFs or screenshots.</li>
</ul>

<h2>6. Your flat: the lease, the deposit and the bills</h2>
<p>If you live in University housing, the handover is in <a href="/guides/graduate-procedures">Graduating</a>. If you rent through Ejar:</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Give notice.</strong> A contract that renews automatically needs 60 days' written notice from either side. If you want to leave before the contract ends, both sides have to agree: either of you starts a termination request in Ejar and the other approves it. Read your contract's early-exit clause first, because many contracts charge one or two months' rent for leaving early.</li>
<li><strong>Agree the move-out inspection.</strong> Walk round the flat with the landlord or agent, take dated photos of every room, and fill in the move-out form in Ejar within seven days of the end date.</li>
<li><strong>Get the deposit back.</strong> The security deposit sits in Ejar's own account, and it is released to you, in full or less the cost of real damage, once the unit is handed over. If the landlord claims damage you did not cause, open a rental dispute from your Ejar dashboard.</li>
<li><strong>Close the utilities.</strong> Pay the final electricity bill through the Saudi Electricity Company's app and ask for the account to be closed or moved to the landlord's name, and do the same with the water bill. Take a photo of the meter readings on the day you leave.</li>
<li><strong>Cancel home internet</strong> if you have a fixed line, and return the router.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote><p>Tip: Ejar's helpline is 199011. Nothing in Ejar needs the landlord's goodwill if the contract and the move-out form are filled in properly.</p></blockquote>

<h2>7. Selling, giving and throwing away</h2>
<p>Everything you own falls into four piles: take, ship, sell or give.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sell.</strong> Students sell to the students who are arriving. Post in your building's or college's group with photos and a price, and on Haraj (haraj.com.sa), the national classifieds site, which charges the seller a small commission on sales made through it. The second-hand furniture shops at the end of Imam Bukhari Street buy and sell used furniture, and pickup trucks for hire wait along the main roads for moving large items. Sell big things first (fridge, washing machine, beds), because they take longest.</li>
<li><strong>Give.</strong> Books in good condition are welcome in masjid libraries and with students in your college. Clothes and household items go to the charity collection bins around the city.</li>
<li><strong>Take payment safely.</strong> Prefer a bank transfer that you see arrive before the item leaves your flat. Do not accept cheques.</li>
</ul>

<h2>8. Shipping your books and belongings</h2>
<p>Books are heavy and bulky, and most students have more of them than any airline allows. Shipping them is normal, and the University's graduation allowance exists for exactly this. The full picture, with carriers and customs, is in <a href="/guides/shipping-and-receiving-parcels">Shipping and receiving parcels in Madinah</a>. The essentials:</p>
<table><thead><tr><th>Option</th><th>Suits</th><th>What to know</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Saudi Post (SPL) international parcel: Economy, Express or Sea Freight</td><td>Boxes of up to 30 kg each</td><td>Economy is delivered to the nearest post office at the other end, Express and Sea Freight to the door. Price depends on destination and on the greater of the real and the volumetric weight. Quote on the SPL price calculator or app. Customer service 19992</td></tr>
<tr><td>International couriers: Aramex, DHL, FedEx, Naqel, SMSA, UPS</td><td>Fast delivery, tracked to the door</td><td>Priced per kg or by volume; each has an online quote tool. Faster than post and usually dearer per kilo</td></tr>
<tr><td>Cargo and freight agents (air or sea)</td><td>Many boxes, or furniture</td><td>Sea freight is the cheapest per kilo for large loads and the slowest, often one to two months. Get at least three quotes in writing and ask what is included at the destination (customs clearance, delivery to the door)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Excess baggage on your flight</td><td>One or two extra bags</td><td>Often cheaper than shipping for a single extra bag. Prepay online, because airport rates are higher</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p><strong>Packing books.</strong> Use small, strong, double-walled boxes and keep each one at 20 to 25 kg, because a 30 kg box of books breaks and hurts whoever carries it. Put the heaviest books flat at the bottom, fill gaps with clothes, tape every seam, and write your name, phone number and destination address on two sides and inside the lid. Make a list of what is in each box with an honest value: you declare it on the customs form, and the same list is your claim if a box is lost.</p>
<p><strong>Customs.</strong> Leaving Saudi Arabia, ordinary used books and personal effects are not on the list of things travellers must declare. Arriving at home, printed books carry no customs duty in most countries, including the UK and the US, and the UK charges no VAT on printed books. Each country has its own threshold for duty-free personal parcels and its own paperwork for people moving back, so check your own country's customs site before the first box leaves.</p>
<blockquote><p>Tip: Weigh a full box on a bathroom scale before you seal it. Carriers charge the greater of the real weight and the volumetric weight (length by width by height in centimetres divided by 5,000), so a half-empty large box costs as much as a full one.</p></blockquote>

<h2>9. Your flight and your bags</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Where you can fly to.</strong> Madinah's Prince Mohammad bin Abdulaziz International Airport has direct flights to dozens of countries, with the thickest schedules to Cairo, Istanbul, Dubai, Doha, Karachi, Jakarta and Kuala Lumpur, and seasonal flights to Europe. If your University ticket must end in your capital city, a connection in Jeddah or Riyadh is normal.</li>
<li><strong>Baggage on the University ticket.</strong> One cabin bag and two checked bags, within the airline's own weight limits. On Saudia's Guest class that is normally 23 kg per bag, and a bag between 23 and 32 kg pays an excess fee per piece. Low-cost airlines charge per kilo over the allowance. Prepay any extra bag online; the airport desk charges more.</li>
<li><strong>Getting to the airport.</strong> Route 400 of Madinah Bus runs round the clock between the Prophet's Mosque area and the airport for a few riyals, paid in the app or by card. A ride app from the Haram or the University takes 25 to 40 minutes.</li>
<li><strong>In hand luggage.</strong> Passport, final exit printout or Visitor ID screenshot, ticket, your degree and legalised documents, your ijazat, the bank closure letter, and a copy of your Ejar contract and move-out form.</li>
</ul>

<h2>10. Goodbyes and what to keep</h2>
<ul>
<li>Visit your teachers before the last week. Ask for a tazkiyah (a letter of recommendation) from those who taught you, and keep the contact details of your mashayikh, your college office and your friends.</li>
<li>Make a last visit to the Prophet's Mosque with time to spare, give salam at the grave, pray in Quba, and visit al-Baqi' if you are a man.</li>
<li>Keep for life: your degree and the legalised copies, the transcript, every ijazah, your student card photo, your University number, your Tawasul login, the bank closure letter and your final exit printout. Employers and universities can check your degree on the University's verification service later.</li>
<li>Tell the people who helped you that you are leaving, and leave your furniture and books with the students who come after you.</li>
</ul>
<p>May Allah accept your years in the city of His Messenger ﷺ and bring you back to it.</p>
