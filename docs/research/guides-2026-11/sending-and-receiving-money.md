# NEW GUIDE: Sending money home and receiving money

Proposed slug: `sending-and-receiving-money`
Stage: `getting-settled`
Title: Sending money home and receiving money in Madinah
Summary: How remittances work under the Saudi Central Bank's rules, the apps and banks students use, what to compare, how to receive money from your family abroad, and the limits that apply to Visitor ID accounts.

The existing FAQ `send-money-home` lists the apps. This guide explains the system around them.

## What is missing today

- The rule that remittances go only through licensed banks and remittance centres, registered to your ID.
- What the fee guide of February 2026 means for students (capped basic fees, VAT added).
- The practical comparison: fee, exchange rate, speed, the amount the family receives.
- Receiving money from abroad: IBAN, SWIFT, the "who pays the charges" field, why the amount arrives short.
- Visitor ID accounts: what stops when the ID expires, and what to do before a trip home.
- Cash: the declaration threshold when flying with money.
- Zakat and sadaqah: Ehsan and Zakaty, including zakat al-fitr.
- Scams: fake "transfer agents" in student groups.

## READY-TO-PASTE DRAFT

```html
<p>Checked 9 November 2026. Money leaves and enters Saudi Arabia only through banks and licensed remittance centres, and every transfer is tied to the ID of the person sending it. Once you have a Saudi account or wallet in your name, sending money home takes a minute in an app.</p>

<h2>Who can send, and from what</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Iqama holders</strong> use their bank's app, a remittance centre's membership, or a licensed wallet. The membership or account is linked to your iqama number.</li>
<li><strong>Visitor ID holders</strong> can open a bank account and get a mada card since September 2025, and transfer from it while the Visitor ID is active. The account is frozen when the Visitor ID expires, so finish any transfer before a trip home and reactivate the ID when you return.</li>
<li><strong>Visitors on a visit visa</strong> without a Saudi account use a remittance centre in person with their passport, or send from their home bank.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where students send from</h2>
<p>Services in common use, in alphabetical order: Alinma, Barq, D360, Enjaz (Bank Albilad), Mobily Pay, SNB NEO, STC Bank, Tahweel Al Rajhi, Tiqmo and urpay. All of them are licensed by the Saudi Central Bank. Each one supports different countries and payout methods (bank account, mobile wallet, cash pick-up), so the right one depends on where your family is.</p>
<table><thead><tr><th>Compare</th><th>Why it matters</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>The amount your family receives</td><td>This is the only number that matters. Type the same riyal amount into two or three apps and compare the final figure in your home currency.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Transfer fee</td><td>Usually a flat fee per transfer, plus 15% VAT on the fee. The Saudi Central Bank caps the fees banks charge for basic services, and the cap took effect on 20 February 2026.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Exchange rate</td><td>Apps build their margin into the rate. A low fee with a poor rate costs more on large amounts.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Speed</td><td>Wallet-to-wallet payouts are often instant; bank account payouts take hours to two working days; SWIFT transfers two to five working days.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Limits</td><td>Each app sets daily and monthly limits, and asks for the purpose of the transfer. Transfers that do not fit your declared income and purpose are held for checks.</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<blockquote><p>Tip: The riyal is fixed at 3.75 to the US dollar. Rates against other currencies move every day, so students sending a fixed sum each month pick a day in the week and send on it rather than watching the rate.</p></blockquote>

<h2>Sending step by step</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Open the app and add a beneficiary: full name as on their ID, bank and account number or IBAN, or their wallet number. Save them once; the app remembers.</li>
<li>Enter the riyal amount and the purpose (family support is the normal choice for students).</li>
<li>Check the fee, the rate and the final amount on the confirmation screen, then confirm with the code sent to your phone.</li>
<li>Keep the receipt in the app until your family confirms the money has arrived.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Receiving money from abroad</h2>
<p>Your family or a scholarship body sends to your Saudi account with two pieces of information: your IBAN (24 characters starting with SA, shown in your banking app) and your bank's SWIFT code. Give them your name exactly as the bank holds it.</p>
<ul>
<li>The sender chooses who pays the charges. "OUR" means the sender pays all fees and you receive the full amount; "SHA" splits them, and an intermediary bank deducts its fee on the way, so the amount lands short by 50 to 150 SAR. Ask the sender to choose OUR for small amounts.</li>
<li>Saudi banks credit incoming transfers within one to two working days of receipt. Your bank asks for the purpose of a large or unusual incoming transfer; a scholarship letter or a family message is enough.</li>
<li>Wallets have IBANs too. Several wallets changed banks in 2025 and 2026 and issued new IBANs, so always send your family the IBAN shown in the app today, not one from an old message.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Cash</h2>
<p>Carrying cash, gold or bearer instruments worth 60,000 SAR or more into or out of the Kingdom must be declared to customs. Below that nothing is declared. Exchange shops around the Haram change most currencies, and the rate on the board is the rate you get.</p>

<h2>Zakat and sadaqah</h2>
<p>Zakat al-mal and sadaqah to Saudi charities go through the national platforms Ehsan (ehsan.sa) and Zakaty, which accept Apple Pay, mada and cards. Ehsan also receives zakat al-fitr in the last days of Ramadan: you choose the region and the number of people and pay the announced rate per person, which follows the price of a sa' of the staple food that year. See <a href="/guides/ramadan-in-madinah">Ramadan in Madinah</a>.</p>

<h2>What goes wrong</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>"Transfer agents" in student groups</strong> who offer a better rate and take your riyals in cash are unlicensed, and you have no protection if the money does not arrive. Use your own account.</li>
<li><strong>A blocked transfer</strong> usually means the app wants proof of the purpose or of your income. Upload your scholarship letter or stipend statement in the app.</li>
<li><strong>A frozen account</strong> after your Visitor ID or iqama expired: renew the ID or iqama and the bank unfreezes it. Nothing is lost.</li>
</ul>
```
