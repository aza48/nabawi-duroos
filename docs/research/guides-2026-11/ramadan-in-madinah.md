## READY-TO-PASTE DRAFT

```html
<p>Checked 9 November 2026. Ramadan turns Madinah's day upside down. The city sleeps late, the Prophet's Mosque fills from Asr, iftar is served to hundreds of thousands in the courtyards, and the last ten nights draw crowds larger than any other time of year except Hajj. Ramadan 1448 begins around 8 February 2027. This page tells you what changes and how to plan for it.</p>

<h2>The Prophet's Mosque in Ramadan</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Taraweeh</strong> follows Isha and is prayed as 10 rak'ahs with 3 of witr, led by the mosque's imams in turn. The schedule of imams is published by the General Authority before the month starts.</li>
<li><strong>Tahajjud (qiyam)</strong> is added in the last ten nights, starting after midnight and ending before suhoor, and the mosque stays open through the night.</li>
<li><strong>Iftar</strong> is laid out on long plastic sheets (sufra) in the courtyards and inside: dates, water, yoghurt, bread and coffee, offered by families and charities under permit. Sit down anywhere, eat what is in front of you, and help fold the sheet before Maghrib prayer. The Authority serves tens of millions of meals in the Two Holy Mosques each Ramadan.</li>
<li><strong>The Rawdah</strong> has a Ramadan timetable with men's and women's slots spread over the day and night, and a different timetable in the last ten days. Book in Nusuk as usual; permits go within minutes of release, so book the moment the Ramadan slots open.</li>
<li><strong>Crowd levels</strong> for each prayer area and the courtyards are published live by the Authority (alharamain.gov.sa, prayer areas). Check it before you leave the house on the 27th night.</li>
<li><strong>Bags</strong>: the free left-luggage centre keeps bags for up to four hours. Prayer mats and small bags are allowed in; large bags and food are not.</li>
</ul>
<blockquote><p>Tip: For Jumu'ah and the odd nights, be inside the mosque an hour before the adhan for Isha, or you will pray in the streets around it. The streets are carpeted and have speakers, and that is where most people end up on the 27th night.</p></blockquote>

<h2>I'tikaf</h2>
<p>I'tikaf in the Prophet's Mosque runs from 20 Ramadan to the end of the month and needs a permit. Registration opens in the first days of Ramadan (it opened on 3 Ramadan 1447) on the Authority's e-services portal (eservices.alharamain.gov.sa), with Nafath for citizens and residents. Applicants must be 18 or over; places are limited and successful applicants receive an SMS. Women have their own designated area. Students on a Visitor ID should try the portal with their Absher login as soon as registration opens.</p>

<h2>The University in Ramadan</h2>
<ul>
<li>Lectures start at 10:00 and are shortened: 50-minute lectures become 35-minute slots (10:00, 10:40, 11:20, 12:00), with breaks around Dhuhr and Asr. The timetable is posted on the University's channels in the days before Ramadan.</li>
<li>The Eid al-Fitr holiday in 1448 runs from 26 February 2027 (19 Ramadan) to 13 March 2027, so classes stop before the last ten nights. The Founding Day holiday is 19 to 22 February 2027.</li>
<li>The mat'am changes to iftar and suhoor service. Book your meals for the month as usual on restaurant.iu.edu.sa.</li>
<li>Free University buses to the Haram run in the evening; ask your building's supervisor for the Ramadan times.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Daily life</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Shops and restaurants</strong> close for the afternoon and open after taraweeh until suhoor. Supermarkets open through the day. Do your shopping late at night when the city is awake.</li>
<li><strong>Offices</strong>: government offices and banks work shorter hours, usually from 10:00 to 15:00 or 16:00. Absher, Nafath and the banking apps do not change.</li>
<li><strong>Transport</strong>: Madinah Bus runs free shuttle routes from park-and-ride hubs across the city to the Prophet's Mosque and to Quba, from 3 pm until an hour after taraweeh, and in the last ten days until half an hour after tahajjud. Ride-app prices surge after taraweeh; walk the first ten minutes away from the Haram before you book.</li>
<li><strong>Eating in public</strong> in daylight is not done. Non-fasting travellers and children eat indoors.</li>
<li><strong>Prices</strong>: hotel prices around the Haram are at their highest in the last ten nights, and dates, sweets and Ramadan foods have their own markets, including the Ramadan night markets around the city.</li>
<li><strong>Umrah in Ramadan</strong> carries the reward of Hajj, so the trains and buses to Makkah are full. Book the train and the Nusuk permit weeks ahead. See <a href="/guides/umrah-rawdah-quba">Umrah, the Rawdah and Quba from Madinah</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Zakat al-fitr</h2>
<p>Zakat al-fitr is one sa' (about three kilograms) of the staple food for each member of the household, paid before the Eid prayer. In Madinah it is given as rice or dates to poor families, or paid in money at the rate announced each year by the national donation platform Ehsan (ehsan.sa), which takes the payment per person and region in the last days of Ramadan. The rate follows the price of rice and dates that year.</p>

<h2>Eid</h2>
<ul>
<li>Eid prayer in the Prophet's Mosque is held shortly after sunrise; the Ministry of Islamic Affairs publishes the exact time for Madinah the day before (6:40 am for Eid al-Fitr 1447). Arrive an hour early with your mat and expect to pray in the courtyards.</li>
<li>Eid prayers are also held in the large masjids and open prayer grounds in every district, which are far less crowded.</li>
<li>Shops reopen on the afternoon of the first day. The University's Eid holiday continues for about two weeks after Eid.</li>
</ul>
<p>Details that change every year: the Rawdah Ramadan timetable, the shuttle routes, the i'tikaf registration date and the Eid prayer time. Check the Authority's channels and the Madinah Bus app in Sha'ban.</p>
```
