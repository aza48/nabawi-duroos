## READY-TO-PASTE DRAFT

```html
<p>Checked 9 November 2026. Deliveries in Saudi Arabia run on the National Address, and sending things abroad runs on weight. Get the address right once and both directions become easy.</p>

<h2>Your National Address and short address</h2>
<p>Every parcel delivered inside the Kingdom has to carry a National Address since 1 January 2026, and banks, Absher and government services use it too. Registering is free.</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Open the Saudi Post (SPL) app or address.gov.sa and sign in with Nafath. Students on a Visitor ID sign in the same way once Absher and Nafath are active.</li>
<li>Choose My National Addresses, then Add, pin your building on the map and enter the building number, unit number and floor.</li>
<li>Confirm, then download the proof of address PDF. Banks and the University accept it.</li>
<li>Note your short address: four letters and four digits that stand for the whole address. Give it to couriers and online shops.</li>
</ol>
<p>If you move, update the address in the app, because the short address changes with it. More on the short address in <a href="/ask/short-address">What is a short address and how do I find mine?</a>.</p>

<h2>Receiving deliveries</h2>
<ul>
<li>Couriers active in Madinah include Aramex, DHL, FedEx, Naqel, SMSA, SPL and UPS, and online shops choose the courier for you. All of them message you with a tracking link and call before arriving. Keep your Saudi number on and answer unknown numbers on delivery day.</li>
<li>Give the courier a landmark in the delivery note. Drivers find the building by map, then look for the flat number and your name on the door.</li>
<li>If you miss a delivery, the app lets you choose a new day or a pick-up point. SPL Economy parcels wait at the post office nearest to your address.</li>
<li>Students in University housing use the campus address and collect from the post office on campus or from the building supervisor, depending on the courier. Ask your building's mushrif how parcels are handled there.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Ordering from abroad: what customs charges</h2>
<table><thead><tr><th>Shipment</th><th>Customs duty</th><th>VAT</th><th>Fees</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Personal order worth less than 1,000 SAR including shipping and insurance</td><td>None</td><td>15% on the full value</td><td>15 SAR declaration processing fee</td></tr>
<tr><td>Personal order worth 1,000 SAR or more</td><td>Duty at the tariff rate for the goods, 5% for many items and more for some</td><td>15% on value plus duty</td><td>A customs service fee of 0.15% of the value, minimum 15 SAR, maximum 500 SAR</td></tr>
<tr><td>Printed books</td><td>None</td><td>15%</td><td>As above</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>The courier pays these at the border and collects them from you before delivery, usually by a payment link. Food supplements, medicines, drones and radio equipment need permits and are often held, so check the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority's list before ordering.</p>
<blockquote><p>Tip: Splitting one order into several parcels to stay under 1,000 SAR is treated as one shipment when the parcels arrive together. Order what you need and budget the 15%.</p></blockquote>

<h2>Sending parcels abroad</h2>
<p>Three kinds of service exist, and all of them price by the greater of the real weight and the volumetric weight (length by width by height in centimetres, divided by 5,000).</p>
<table><thead><tr><th>Service</th><th>Limit per box</th><th>Delivered to</th><th>Good for</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>SPL Economy</td><td>30 kg</td><td>The post office nearest the receiver</td><td>Books and clothes that are not urgent</td></tr>
<tr><td>SPL Express and Express Plus</td><td>30 kg</td><td>The receiver's door</td><td>Faster, tracked, priced by destination; Express Plus is the premium option at a fixed price per box</td></tr>
<tr><td>SPL Sea Freight</td><td>30 kg</td><td>The receiver's door</td><td>The lowest price per kilo for heavy boxes, the longest wait</td></tr>
<tr><td>Couriers: Aramex, DHL, FedEx, Naqel, SMSA, UPS</td><td>Varies, usually 30 to 70 kg</td><td>The door</td><td>Speed and tracking; each has an online quote tool</td></tr>
<tr><td>Freight agents (air or sea cargo)</td><td>By the cubic metre or pallet</td><td>Port, airport or door, as agreed</td><td>Many boxes or furniture</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Get a quote on the SPL price calculator or app, and from two or three couriers, for the same box size and weight. Prices change by country and season, so compare on the day.</li>
<li>Pack in strong double-walled boxes. Keep each box of books at 20 to 25 kg. Write the full destination address, the receiver's phone number and your own on two sides and inside the lid.</li>
<li>Make a list of the contents with an honest value for each box. You enter it on the customs declaration, and it is your claim if the box is lost.</li>
<li>Take the boxes to an SPL branch or arrange a courier collection from your door. Bring your iqama or passport with your Visitor ID.</li>
<li>Keep the receipt and tracking number until the receiver has the box in hand.</li>
</ol>
<p>Prohibited in the post: cash, jewellery, lithium batteries on their own, aerosols, perfume in quantity, medicines that need a prescription, and anything the destination country bans. SPL's customer service is 19992.</p>
<blockquote><p>Tip: Dates travel well in sealed boxes and are a favourite gift, but some countries ask for a food declaration. Zamzam water flies as checked baggage in its sealed 5-litre box on most airlines from Saudi Arabia, and does not go in the post.</p></blockquote>

<h2>Customs at the other end</h2>
<p>Each country taxes incoming parcels by its own rules. Printed books carry no customs duty in most countries, and the UK charges no VAT on them. Used personal belongings sent by someone moving home are often relieved of duty if they are declared as such, with a form filed before the boxes arrive. Check your own country's customs site for "personal effects" or "transfer of residence" before the first box leaves Madinah, and give the receiver the list of contents.</p>

<h2>Luggage and the Haramain train</h2>
<p>The train allows one bag of up to 25 kg and 65 by 55 by 35 cm, plus a small hand bag. Anything over is turned away at the security check and sent to the SPL counter in the station, which ships it to the destination station for collection the next day for a fee of about 75 to 150 SAR a bag. If you are moving between Madinah and Makkah or Jeddah with more than one bag, send the extra box by SPL a day ahead instead.</p>
```
