# NEW GUIDE: Visiting family: bringing your parents to Madinah

Proposed slug: `bringing-parents-on-a-visit`
Stage: `family`
Title: Bringing your parents to Madinah: visas, their first week and looking after them
Summary: The three visa routes for parents (tourist eVisa, Umrah visa through Nusuk, family visit visa for iqama holders), what each costs and allows, extending a stay, where they sleep, the Rawdah and wheelchair help, health cover, and how to make the visit easy for older parents.

`visas-for-family-and-visitors` covers the visa rules in general. This guide is written from the student's side: my parents are coming, what do I do, in order.

## What is missing today

- Which route fits which parent: nationality eligible for the eVisa or not, student with iqama or Visitor ID.
- The Umrah visa through Nusuk as the route for nationalities outside the eVisa list.
- That the family visit visa needs a sponsor with a work iqama, which a student does not hold, so the University route or the eVisa or Umrah visa apply.
- Extension mechanics: 7 days before expiry, insurance, the 180-day cap.
- Their first week: airport, hotel near the Haram or your flat, Rawdah permit for older people, wheelchairs and the free carts, women's gates for a mother.
- Health: insurance included in the eVisa, hospitals, medicines from home.
- Money: their foreign cards work; cash exchange.
- Heat and timing: when to bring them.

<p>Checked 9 November 2026. Parents visit for three reasons: to see you, to pray in the Prophet's Mosque and to do Umrah. The visa comes first, and which one depends on their passport and on your own status. Then plan their stay around their strength, the weather and the prayer times.</p>

<h2>Which visa</h2>
<table><thead><tr><th>Route</th><th>Who it fits</th><th>Cost and length</th><th>Umrah</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Tourist eVisa (visa.visitsaudi.com)</td><td>Parents from the eligible countries, including the UK, the US, Canada, Australia, the EU and others; holders of a valid UK, US or Schengen visa or residence from other countries</td><td>About 480 to 535 SAR with the compulsory insurance; one year, multiple entries, up to 90 days in total</td><td>Yes, with a Nusuk permit. Not Hajj</td></tr>
<tr><td>Umrah visa through Nusuk (nusuk.sa)</td><td>Parents from any country, including those not on the eVisa list</td><td>Government fee about 300 SAR plus insurance; issued with a Nusuk package or through a licensed agent; a multiple-entry version valid a year exists since 2026</td><td>Yes. That is its purpose</td></tr>
<tr><td>Family visit visa (visa.mofa.gov.sa)</td><td>Parents of a resident who holds a work iqama. Students' iqamas are not work iqamas, so students go through the University instead</td><td>300 SAR single entry or 500 SAR multiple, plus insurance; single entry allows up to 90 days</td><td>Yes, with a Nusuk permit</td></tr>
<tr><td>Through the University</td><td>Scholarship students with an iqama can ask the Deanship of Student Affairs about visitor permits for parents; the windows open and close during the year</td><td>Set by the University</td><td>Yes, with a Nusuk permit</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<blockquote><p>Tip: Apply only on the official sites. Agents who "help" with the eVisa add their own fee to the same form you can fill in yourself in twenty minutes.</p></blockquote>
<p>Passports must have six months left on the day of entry. Older parents who take regular medicines should bring a doctor's letter and a month's supply; pharmacies here will not accept a foreign prescription, but a Saudi doctor can write a new one. Controlled medicines need a traveller's permit from the Food and Drug Authority before the flight: see <a href="/ask/bringing-medicine-into-saudi">Bringing medicine into Saudi Arabia</a>.</p>

<h2>Extending their stay</h2>
<ul>
<li>The tourist eVisa cannot be extended inside the Kingdom. A new one is issued after the old one expires, and the 90 days in a year are counted across all entries.</li>
<li>A family visit visa is extended on Absher by the host, from 7 days before it expires, with new medical insurance for the extra period and a fee of about 100 SAR each time. The total stay cannot pass 180 days from entry.</li>
<li>Overstaying is fined, and the fine is paid before the person can leave. Put the expiry date in your phone.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Their first days</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Airport.</strong> Meet them in arrivals. Route 400 of Madinah Bus runs round the clock to the Prophet's Mosque area for a few riyals, and a ride app takes 25 to 40 minutes to most districts. Wheelchairs are free at the airport; ask the airline when booking.</li>
<li><strong>Where they sleep.</strong> Hotels within walking distance of the Haram save tired parents the daily taxi. Prices double in Ramadan and around Hajj and are lowest in summer. If they stay with you in a flat, check that your contract allows guests; University housing does not allow overnight guests.</li>
<li><strong>Their SIM.</strong> A tourist SIM is sold at the airport and in the malls against the passport. Or share your hotspot.</li>
<li><strong>Money.</strong> Foreign cards work in almost every shop and taxi. Exchange shops near the Haram change cash at the board rate.</li>
<li><strong>Nusuk.</strong> Register them in Nusuk with their passport and visa details, then book the Rawdah permit and, if they are going to Makkah, the Umrah permit.</li>
</ol>

<h2>At the Prophet's Mosque with older parents</h2>
<ul>
<li>Book the Rawdah permit for them in Nusuk; one permit per person is issued every 365 days, so pick the day with care, and book your mother's slot in the women's times. Older people and wheelchair users have their own route to the Rawdah, and the stewards help.</li>
<li>Wheelchairs are lent free inside the mosque, and electric carts carry people who cannot walk the distances. Ask at the gates; the helpers are marked.</li>
<li>Take them before Fajr or after Isha in summer, and in the daytime in winter. Shade and water coolers are everywhere, but the marble courtyards are hot by mid-morning from May to September.</li>
<li>Your mother enters through the women's gates on the northern side; the women's sections, the Rawdah times for women and the carts are in <a href="/guides/women-at-masjid-an-nabawi">Masjid an-Nabawi for sisters and families</a>.</li>
<li>Al-Baqi' is open to men after Fajr and after Asr. Quba is a short taxi ride, and Uhud and Qiblatayn make a gentle afternoon.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Umrah with parents</h2>
<p>The Haramain train from Madinah takes about two and a half hours to Makkah and is the easiest journey for older people: no road, a seat, a toilet, and help with bags at the station. Book early, enter ihram at Dhul Hulayfah on the way (the station has no miqat stop, so change into ihram before boarding, or do so on the train before it passes the miqat), and book the Umrah permit in Nusuk for the day. Wheelchairs and pushers for tawaf and sa'i are available in the Grand Mosque. The full route is in <a href="/guides/umrah-rawdah-quba">Umrah, the Rawdah and Quba from Madinah</a>.</p>

<h2>If they fall ill</h2>
<p>The eVisa and the Umrah visa include medical insurance for emergencies; keep the policy number on your phone. For anything else, private hospitals and clinics see walk-in patients and charge 100 to 300 SAR for a consultation. In an emergency call 997 for an ambulance. Hospitals, pharmacies and the health apps are in <a href="/guides/doctors-hospitals-and-pharmacies">Doctors, hospitals, pharmacies and emergency numbers</a>.</p>

<h2>When to bring them</h2>
<p>November to February is cool and busy; late September to mid-November and late February to March are mild and calmer. Ramadan is unforgettable and crowded, with hotel prices at their highest. Summer is cheapest and hottest, and fine for parents who can stay indoors between Dhuhr and Asr. See <a href="/guides/seasons-and-camping">Madinah by season</a>.</p>
