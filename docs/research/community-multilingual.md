# Community and multilingual research: studying at the Islamic University of Madinah (IU) and living in Madinah

Researched 7 November 2026 for MESH (medinahub.org). Written in plain English; all non-English material is translated and paraphrased, nothing is quoted at length.

## 0. Method, coverage and what was blocked

What I could actually read: official IU and Saudi Press Agency pages, the Australian students' site (ASMU), the Canadian students' blog, Indonesian press and student writing (Kompas, Saudinesia, PPMI Madinah), a Russian agency site (iuksa.ru) with a long review wall, Bengali and Turkish news and forum snippets, French agency blogs, Urdu and Arabic forum archives, Quora and IslamicBoard snippets.

What was blocked or empty:
- Reddit: the search tool refuses the reddit.com domain, so no Reddit threads were read. A general search returned none either.
- ahlalhdeeth.com (Ahl al-Hadith forum): connection refused when fetching. I only got search snippets and Maktaba Shamela archive titles. Those threads are about 2009 to 2018, so they are old.
- X (Twitter) posts by Study in Saudi: fetch returns HTTP 402. I only have search-snippet text of two posts (one on bringing a wife and children, Jan 2025; one by a graduate on what comes after graduation, mid 2026).
- IU FAQ page and the Indonesian and French "admission guide 1446" PDFs: the FAQ loads dynamically (came back empty), the PDFs came back as unreadable binary.
- Telegram, Snapchat, WhatsApp, TikTok and YouTube content: not fetchable. I only saw titles in search results.
- Hausa and Somali: nothing useful about IU, only generic English aggregator pages. Turkish: only 2023 forum threads with no answers.
- Honest limitation: there is very little genuinely recent (2024 to 2026) first-person student writing on the open web. Most 2024 to 2026 material is press, agencies and scholarship aggregators. Student-experience material is mostly 2010 to 2023. Every fact below carries a date and a confidence level.

"Seen" below means the number of distinct sources, out of roughly 30 I read or saw snippets of, where the question was asked or answered. It is a rough signal, not a statistic.

## 2. Pain points and mistakes (with sources)

Admission and waiting
1. Waiting is long and opaque. The Russian agency's review wall (https://iuksa.ru/reviews/, entries dated 2022 to Jan 2025) shows waits from about six months to three years: one applicant applied in 2019 and heard in 2022; another applied in 2018, was accepted in 2021 and could not travel in time. Older Urdu and Arabic forum threads (2017 to 2018) say applications from old cycles may expire, be rejected or roll over, so keep checking status. ASMU (2023) says results usually come around July, in batches.
2. Intermediaries vanish after acceptance. Four separate reviews (2022 and 2024) describe a "brother" or firm who submitted documents then went silent for weeks once the applicant was accepted. One had to pay for a separate consultation to finish visa steps (same URL; it is a competitor agency's page, so weigh it). It matches the official line that IU has no authorised agents and that applying is free.
3. Documents photographed on a phone are rejected by some checkers; use clear colour scans (iuksa.ru, 30 Aug 2026).
4. Translation and attestation mistakes cost months: one student accepted in 2021 missed the start because certificate and birth certificate legalisation was incomplete (iuksa.ru reviews, Feb 2024); a missing passport photo cost time and money (Nov 2022).
5. Late arrival is discouraged and can be refused (ASMU). The gap between acceptance and term start can be only about four weeks, so start attestation and the visa early (ASMU; Study in Saudi guide, May 2026).
6. Over-age or old-certificate applicants waste a cycle. Rule repeated in every language: under 25 at the start, certificate not older than five years, exceptions rare (ASMU, IslamicBoard 2018, Urdu forum, sapamuslim.com). Verify each cycle.
7. Scholarship aggregator sites (dozens of "Islamic University of Madinah Scholarship 2026/2027" pages) give conflicting ages (16, 17 to 23, 17 to 25), deadlines and stipends. Students are being misled by them.

Arrival and daily life
8. The stipend does not start immediately. ASMU says allowances begin after the first month or two and advises saving cash for meal-hall charges and fees (page copyright 2023).
9. Halls: two per room in newer buildings, up to four in older ones (up to six per a 2010 Canadian blog), with partitions, shared bathrooms, floor kitchens and laundry. One 2024 Kompas report notes one dorm was messy after the housing management company changed. Theft from rooms is a recurring warning (2010), so use lockers.
10. Married students are the most stressed group. Long separation caused some to fail or leave (Saudinesia, Dec 2023). University family housing is for postgraduates per ASMU (Feb 2017), so undergraduates rent off campus at their own cost. A Jan 2025 student post describes a family residence route from the third semester with a refundable deposit (see section 3).
11. Weak Arabic from staying in a national bubble: Saudinesia (Dec 2023) and a 2022 personal account (about 2007 to 2013) agree that speaking with Arabs and a classical-Arabic partner beats merely being in Madinah.
12. Phones, games and social media, careless friendships, and skipping the Nabawi halaqat and never meeting senior scholars (Saudinesia, Dec 2023).
13. Side jobs (guiding pilgrims, airport and hotel work) eat study time (Saudinesia, Dec 2023). The legal status of such work is not answered anywhere I found.
14. Buying a car in the first year, especially from a seller who hides defects. Wait, and buy from a trusted graduating student (Canadian blog, 2010; still often repeated).
15. Failing courses lowers GPA, harms master's chances and can mean being sent home (Canadian blog).
16. Burnout from comparing yourself with advanced students; extremes of over-restricting or neglecting (Canadian blog, 2010). A mid-2026 graduate post says far fewer than half who start finish.
17. A semester off often means not returning (ASMU).
18. Hajj places for students are limited, about once in five years per one Indonesian report (Kompas, May 2024).
19. Unused yearly flight tickets lapse rather than being paid in cash (Indonesian summaries); a 2010 student blog says tickets cannot be carried over. Moderate confidence on the cash part.
20. Tourist-style scams near the Prophet's Mosque (staged sob stories, taxi overcharging, counterfeit zamzam outside official counters) can hit students too (theislamicinformation.com, hajjsafe.com, undated). No Madinah-specific student rental scam was found; MESH's flat checklist already covers the risks.

What people wish they had known (repeated themes)
- A personal shaykh or senior mentor from day one (former-student reflection on chriscaras.com).
- Plan the college choice early; it is the most stressful decision (same source).
- Arrive with cash because the first stipend and rent do not line up (ASMU, Canadian blog).
- Practise spoken Arabic from week one and limit hours spent in English-speaking circles.
- Have a plan for after graduation: a mid-2026 graduate's post suggests teaching Qur'an and Arabic at local masjids and running summer camps (snippet only).

## 5. Suggested new guides and FAQs

### New guides

1. After you are accepted: visa, attestation, ticket, arrival (highest priority, stage getting-started)
   - What "accepted" on Study in Saudi means versus final admission; the Minhati notice and what to print.
   - Study visa steps by country; passport validity; medical.
   - Attestation: which documents, who attests, in what order, translation, clear colour scans.
   - Ticket letter and arrival window; what if you cannot make the start date; deferral up to two years after final acceptance.
   - First weeks: Arabic placement test, medical check, student ID, iqama timeline, first stipend (not immediate, carry cash).
   - Mistakes list: late attestation, photographed documents, agents who vanish.
2. Applying without an agent: the honest guide (stage getting-started)
   - How the portal works, no fee, no authorised agents.
   - Recommendation letters: how many, who, language, what to say.
   - Age and certificate-age rules and exceptions.
   - Realistic waiting times (six months to three years in reports), how to check status, what rolling over means.
   - How to spot aggregator and WhatsApp scams.
3. Life in the halls (stage study)
   - Rooms, roommates, lockers, laundry, kitchens, canteens and prices, shuttle bus, scooters.
   - First-month money plan: stipend delay and meal charges.
   - Theft, internet, the Friday and Saturday rhythm.
4. The Arabic year and the Ma'had: how to survive it (stage study)
   - Placement test, two to four semesters, how to skip a level, study routine, speaking practice outside your nationality group, books (link /library#mahad).
   - Choosing a college: what each leads to, changing college, GPA effects.
5. Married students: the realistic path (stage family)
   - Marriage does not block admission; housing is not provided for most undergraduates.
   - Family routes: visitor visa with periodic exits, university family residency from semester three with refundable deposit (confirm current amount), paperwork.
   - Cost table, link /guides/finding-an-apartment, and what married students wish they had known.
6. Money and work for students (stage getting-settled)
   - Stipend reality in 2026, what covers what, savings buffer.
   - What is and is not allowed on a student iqama (confirm with a named official source before publishing; none found).
   - Side jobs and their study cost, remote work for clients abroad.
7. First-year survival and mentorship (stage study)
   - Finding a senior student or shaykh, routine, burnout and phone drift, the Nabawi halaqat as part of the plan, Hajj and Umrah opportunities, summer-break options.
8. After graduation (stage leaving)
   - A master's is a new competitive application; masjid-based teaching and camps as ideas.
