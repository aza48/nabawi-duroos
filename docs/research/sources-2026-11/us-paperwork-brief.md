# Brief: "Paperwork to finish in the US before you move to Madinah" (guide to write)

On 9 November 2026 the owner sent a student's answer to "For Americans moving to Saudi Arabia and hoping for an iqama, what paperwork do you wish you had done in the US first, to avoid trips back?", and asked for it to be researched online in detail and made into a guide. Slug claimed by naseer: `paperwork-before-you-move-from-the-us` (stage `getting-started`, topic "Start here").

## Claims in the answer, each to check against an official source before publishing
1. Saudi Arabia joined the Hague Apostille Convention, so a US public document with an apostille needs no Saudi embassy legalisation. Check the date it entered into force (HCCH status table) and whether MOFA still stamps anything on arrival.
2. FBI Identity History Summary (federal check, not local police): apply online through the FBI's eDO service (the answer says "eFOIA", which is wrong), fingerprints at a participating USPS location, then an apostille from the US Department of State Office of Authentications. Check whether the electronic PDF can be apostilled, or only the paper copy with the FBI seal and signature.
3. Degrees and transcripts: official copies from the university, notarised by the registrar, apostilled by the Secretary of State of the state where the university is. Check what the Islamic University asks of US applicants (certified copies, attestation, equivalency) in MESH's `applying-to-the-islamic-university` and `docs/research/iu-pdfs-2026-11/`.
4. Civil documents (birth, marriage, divorce): certified copies issued by the state, apostilled by that state's Secretary of State. Needed for family visas, a baby's papers and dependants. Federal documents (FBI, naturalisation) go to the US Department of State.
5. Several apostilled copies of birth and marriage certificates, because Saudi offices keep originals. Check this before stating it.
6. A general or financial power of attorney for someone trusted in the US, notarised (and apostilled if it will be used in Saudi Arabia).
7. Driving: an International Driving Permit from AAA (the only issuer authorised by the State Department, alongside AATA). Whether a US licence can be converted to a Saudi one (US states on the Saudi list; MESH's `drivers-license` guide covers conversion). A certified driving record from the state DMV: check whether Saudi Arabia ever asks for it before saying so.
8. Bank statements, credit reports: likely leave out unless an official Saudi page asks for them (landlords and banks here don't need a US credit report).
9. Business documents (MISA licence: articles of incorporation, good standing, board resolutions, audited accounts with apostille): link the company guide and keep this short. Most MESH readers are students.
10. Translations into Arabic: done by a licensed translation office in Saudi Arabia. Check whether Saudi offices accept translations done abroad.

## The chain to explain simply
Document, then notarised if it's a private document, then an apostille from the Secretary of State (state documents) or the US Department of State (federal documents). Since the Apostille Convention there is no Saudi embassy or MOFA legalisation step for apostilled documents; check the current MOFA rule for documents that still need its stamp.

## Also check
- A medical report and vaccinations for the student visa (see `applying-to-the-islamic-university`).
- Children's school records and vaccination cards for `kindergartens-and-schools`.
- Prescriptions and the controlled medicines permit (`what-to-bring-to-madinah` covers it: link, don't repeat).
- Keep the guide useful to the UK, Canada and others too, with a short line on each where the steps are simple.
