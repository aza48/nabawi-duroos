<p>Checked November 2026. Official papers are far easier to get in the US than from Madinah. Saudi Arabia and the US are both members of the Hague Apostille Convention, so a US document with an apostille is accepted here with no Saudi embassy stamp. This guide sets out which documents to prepare, who certifies each one, and what to pack.</p>
<h2>How the document chain works</h2>
<p>An apostille is a certificate attached to one document that confirms the signature and seal on it are genuine. The Convention has applied between Saudi Arabia and the US since 7 December 2022. Saudi government offices, banks and courts accept an apostille in place of the old route through the Saudi embassy and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (وزارة الخارجية).</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Get an official copy</strong> from the body that issued the document: a certified birth certificate from the state, an official transcript from your school, the FBI result from the FBI.</li>
<li><strong>Notarise it only if it is a private document,</strong> such as a power of attorney or a school's signed certification. Never notarise a public record such as an FBI check or a birth certificate.</li>
<li><strong>Add the apostille.</strong> A state document goes to the Secretary of State of the state that issued it. A federal document, such as the FBI check, goes to the US Department of State's Office of Authentications.</li>
<li><strong>Translate it into Arabic</strong> when a Saudi office asks. The apostille does not replace the translation.</li>
</ol>

<h2>The FBI background check</h2>
<p>The University asks for a police clearance (شهادة خلو من السوابق) from your country. In the US, the record that covers the whole country is the FBI's Identity History Summary. A letter from your local police covers only that force's area.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Apply online</strong> through the FBI's Electronic Departmental Order service (eDO) at <a href="https://www.edo.cjis.gov/">edo.cjis.gov</a> and pay the FBI fee. Keep the confirmation email.</li>
<li><strong>Give your fingerprints</strong> at a Post Office that offers FBI fingerprinting, after you register with USPS for the appointment. Take the confirmation email and a photo ID. Not every Post Office does this, so check the location first.</li>
<li><strong>Ask for a paper copy by post</strong> as well as the electronic result. The FBI prints its watermark and the signature of a division official on the result.</li>
<li><strong>Send the paper copy for the apostille</strong> to the US Department of State's Office of Authentications with form DS-4194, naming Saudi Arabia as the country of use. The fee is 20 US dollars for each document, as of November 2026. By post, allow at least five weeks.</li>
</ol>

<p>The application asks for your secondary school certificate and transcript, your birth certificate, a medical report and Arabic translations of anything not in Arabic. <h2>Papers for your wife and children</h2>
<p>Family visas, school places and a baby's papers all need proof of who is related to whom. Have these certified and apostilled before you leave:</p>
<ul>
<li>Your marriage certificate, and any earlier divorce decree.</li>
<li>Each child's birth certificate. For a family visa, the child's certificate names both parents.</li>
<li>Each child's vaccination record and the school reports for the last two or three years.</li>
</ul>
<p>Order two or three certified copies of each birth and marriage certificate and have each one apostilled. One apostille covers one copy, and ordering a fresh copy from Madinah means international post both ways.</p>
<p>If only one parent is a US citizen, the US Embassy registers a baby born in Saudi Arabia as a US citizen when that parent proves five years of physical presence in the US before the birth, two of them after age 14. The Embassy accepts school transcripts, tax returns with W-2 forms, a Social Security earnings statement and pay slips. A driving licence does not count. Gather these before you move.</p>

<h2>Driving</h2>
<p>In the US, only the American Automobile Association (AAA) and the American Automobile Touring Alliance (AATA) issue International Driving Permits for the State Department. As of November 2026, AAA charges 20 US dollars.</p>
<ul>
<li>You must be 18 or over with a valid US licence. Take two passport photos, signed on the back.</li>
<li>The permit lasts one year and cannot be renewed. It is issued up to six months before the date you want it to start.</li>
<li>It is valid only with your original US licence, so bring both.</li>
<li>A branch usually issues it while you wait. By post, allow two to four weeks.</li>
</ul>
<p>With an iqama, a US licence is swapped for a Saudi one: see <a href="/guides/drivers-license">Getting a Saudi driving licence</a>.</p>
<h2>Someone to act for you at home</h2>
<p>Before you leave, sign a power of attorney before a notary so someone you trust can deal with your bank, post and papers in the US. If it will be used in Saudi Arabia, have it apostilled by the Secretary of State of the state where it was notarised.</p>
<p>If you forget, the US Embassy in Riyadh and the Consulates General in Jeddah and Dhahran notarise documents by appointment only, in person, at 50 US dollars for each seal as of November 2026. Bring the document typed and unsigned, and sign it in front of the officer.</p>
<h2>From the UK, Canada and elsewhere</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>UK:</strong> the police certificate comes from ACRO, and the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office's Legalisation Office adds the apostille.</li>

<li><strong>Other countries:</strong> members of the Convention follow the same chain through their own apostille office. For other countries, documents go through the foreign ministry, then the Saudi embassy there.</li>
