## Conflicts and how they were settled
1. **Is homeschooling legal for expatriate children?** Commercial sources disagree: Expat Exchange and Expatica say it is allowed or tolerated; Legacy Online School calls it a grey area; CambriLearn says the 2026 law requires in-person education and does not distinguish by nationality; vision2030.ai says the word homeschooling does not appear in the law. None is official and the law's text could not be opened. Settled by stating only the confirmed points of the law (compulsory 6 to 15, free government schooling, Ministry action against deprivation, in force January 2027) and telling families to ask the Madinah Education Department and keep the answer in writing. No claim that homeschooling is allowed or banned.
2. **"Education is delivered in person" (CambriLearn)** versus Gulf Business ("classroom instruction stays primary, while e-learning and distance education expand"). Left out.
3. **Government schools and visit visas.** The existing `kindergartens-and-schools` guide says government schools do not take children on a visit visa; the MOE affiliation page speaks of "resident or visitor" pupils. These are different routes (regular enrolment versus affiliation). The guide does not restate the visit visa rule and says to ask whether a child qualifies for affiliation.
4. **Attestation.** Saudi Arabia joined the Apostille Convention (in force 7 December 2022, https://www.hcch.net/en/news-archive/details/?varevent=857), but the MOE school equivalency page speaks of attestation by the foreign ministry or equivalent body in the issuing country. The guide uses the MOE wording and does not mention apostilles.
5. **Placement test categories.** One MOE post lists three categories (never studied; resumed after 3+ years and past regular age; studied and broke off 3+ years); a Taif notice targets people over 21. The guide uses the two widest categories without an age.

## Open questions for the owner (left out of the guide)
1. How the General Education Law and its coming implementing regulations treat home education, and whether the compulsory schooling article applies to non-Saudi children in practice. Needs the Umm al-Qura text (uqn.gov.sa) and the Madinah Education Department's answer.
2. Who qualifies for affiliation (الانتساب) in Madinah: age limits, whether a Visitor ID or visit visa child qualifies, fees, and the registration window. The MOE page refers to "the rules for Saudi affiliate pupils" in chapter one of its admission guide, which could not be opened.
3. Whether a homeschooled expatriate child (as opposed to a child who has been out of school) may sit the placement test, and at what ages.
4. Whether the Ministry makes IGCSE or A Level results taken as a private candidate equivalent for school placement or university entry, and how it treats online school reports.
5. Whether there is a British Council (or other approved) exam venue in Madinah for private candidates, and current fees and deadlines for the January and May/June 2027 series.
6. The 2026 and 2027 Madinah Book Fair dates (not announced in sources found).
7. Whether the King Abdulaziz library complex in Madinah has a public children's section (one secondary source says it serves all ages; not confirmed).
8. Any homeschooling family groups or co-ops in Madinah: none confirmed, and naming one would conflict with the impartiality rule anyway.
9. Network: moe.gov.sa, uqn.gov.sa and sabq.org need to be allowed for WebFetch to check the starred pages directly.

## Review

Reviewed 10 November 2026 against three lenses (facts, house rules, consistency with the site).

### Changes made
1. **Attestation of earlier school papers** (facts, must): now attested by the Ministry of Education (or body running schools) and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in the issuing country, then by the Saudi embassy and its cultural attaché where there is one. "Certified Arabic translation" changed to "certified translation". Source: https://moe.gov.sa/en/reference-and-support/equivalency-and-accreditation/educational-certificates-equivalency (read November 2026). This supersedes the "foreign ministry or equivalent body" row in the table above and conflict 4.
2. **Equivalence procedure** (facts, must): for grades below the final secondary year the Madinah Education Department makes the papers equivalent itself; only a secondary school leaving certificate is sent to the Ministry electronically (Aamali). Same MOE page. Supersedes the "Equivalency procedure" row above.
3. **Edexcel series** (facts, must): October/November series added for Pearson Edexcel IGCSE and IAL, with a link to the British Council registration dates page for each series' deadline. Source: https://saudiarabia.britishcouncil.org/en/exam/igcse-school/register/dates-deadlines
4. **Affiliation wording** (facts, should; site, must): "resident or visitor child" changed to "a child", so the guide no longer contradicts `kindergartens-and-schools` on visit-visa children (see open question 2). Absher line now reads "proof of entry and residence", "four weeks before the first and the second semester exams" (not "at least"), and the exception is a trip forced by circumstances of no more than two weeks.
5. **Equivalence condition** (facts, should): "studied regularly" changed to "attended the school regularly", matching the MOE condition of regular attendance.
6. **Government schools** (site, should): aligned with `kindergartens-and-schools`: children of iqama holders, authorisation letter from the Ministry of Education and a letter from the parent's employer, no children on visit, tourist or Umrah visas.
7. **Birth certificate** (site, should): now says attested and translated into Arabic, matching `kindergartens-and-schools`.
8. **Library locations** (site, should): the repeated gate and bag details replaced by a link to the FAQ `library-bags-laptops`.
9. **Link text** (site, should): "term dates" changed to "registration dates", since `kindergartens-and-schools` has no term dates.
10. **House rules** (should): h3 renamed "Sitting IGCSE and A Levels as a private candidate"; "Many" changed to "Some" families; "certificate equivalency" changed to "certificate equivalence" in the summary and the final step; the slogan line "Arabic opens everything here" replaced with a plain line. The reviewer's suggested replacement ("The Haram circles ... teach in Arabic") was not used, because the facts lens found the circles teach in many languages.

### Facts removed (now open questions)
1. **Language of teaching in the Masjid an-Nabawi halaqat.** "Teaching is in Arabic" removed: help.qm.edu.sa says nothing on language and SPA (https://www.spa.gov.sa/N2231416, 2024 figures) reports circles in 16 languages. The current languages offered for children's circles, and how to choose one at registration, are unconfirmed.
2. **Madinah Book Fair past dates.** The 2024 and 2025 dates were removed; the guide now points to lpt.moc.gov.sa for each year's dates. The 2026 and 2027 dates remain open (open question 6).
3. **Visitor eligibility for affiliation.** The claim that a visitor child can be affiliated was removed; it stays as open question 2, and it conflicts with the existing `kindergartens-and-schools` statement on visit visas until the Madinah Education Department confirms.
