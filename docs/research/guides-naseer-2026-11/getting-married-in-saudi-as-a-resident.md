## Conflicts and how they were settled
1. **Who must take the screening.** MOH (2026 Healthy Marriage Day page) says the programme requires both parties to a contract to submit the certificate. The MOJ Create Marriage Contract page (English and Arabic, modified 18 Oct 2026) says the medical exam is required "for Saudis only". The MOJ "Certify marriage contract" page (paper contract from a ma'dhun, usable when both spouses are non-Saudi) lists "attaching the marriage medical test". Settled: the guide says it is compulsory for Saudis including a Saudi marrying a non-Saudi, and states the MOJ fact that the Najiz contract service lists it for Saudis only when both are non-Saudi. It does not say expat couples are exempt in every route. See open question 1.
2. **Certificate validity for a Saudi/non-Saudi marriage.** MOH pages say six months. Okaz (23 June 2023) reports a Saudi Health Council letter (7-35544, 1/12/1444H) extending validity to one year for marriages where one partner is non-Saudi, and allowing any premarital centre (government or private) instead of designated ones: https://www.okaz.com.sa/news/local/2136869. Single news source, not on an official page I could read. Settled: guide states six months (official MOH), one-year rule left out.
3. **Both parties legally resident.** Only commercial and law-firm sites say both must hold valid residence. Left out (open question 2).
4. **Ma'dhuns for non-Saudi couples.** 2015 to 2017 news (Al-Watan, Okaz, Sabq) says only designated ma'dhuns handle contracts where both parties are non-Saudi. Old; not confirmed as current. Left out (open question 3).
5. **Fees.** Commercial sites give 300 to 2,000 SAR for a ma'dhun and an unofficial attestation fee. Only MOFA's 30 SAR is stated (official). Ma'dhun fee left out.
6. **Result turnaround.** A clinic blog says 7 to 10 days; no official figure. Left out. The guide originally included the official "sample rejected after seven days" rule but it was cut for length.
