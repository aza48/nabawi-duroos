Written in our own words. Nothing is copied from posts. No private names, phone numbers or personal handles are recorded. Public official bodies, public channels and businesses are named where useful (this file is research, not public copy; the impartiality rule applies when anything is turned into site text).

## B. Contradictions with current guides

Guide text is quoted from the repository (src/server/seed/content/guides-2026.ts and guides-student-life.ts).

1. **Schools and visit visas.** Guide `kindergartens-and-schools` says: "many accept children whose parents are on a visit visa, not only iqama holders." One consultant-style summary says registration in local schools needs a residence permit and that families without one often homeschool; HijrahNow does not say either way. Evidence is Low (one aggregator-style source that I could only read through a search summary), but it is opposite to the guide and the guide has no source. Ask admins to confirm from parents. Also, Telegram #411 says istiqdam gives school places for free, which is a separate route.
2. **Istiqdam deposit.** FAQ `istiqdam-deposit` says "The latest amount quoted for the family residency deposit is 30,000 SAR (November 2025). Earlier reports quoted 10,000 SAR (January 2025)." The January 2025 post itself says the deposit was 40,000 before it became 10,000, and the group chat (18 Oct 2026, in docs/research/group-chat-2026.md) says istiqdam is closed to everyone. So three figures (40,000, 10,000, 30,000) and a possible closure. Do not present a deposit as current.
3. **Embassy visit.** Guide in guides-2026.ts says "The visa comes by email, with no embassy visit." The IU page "procedures after final acceptance" still tells students to visit the Saudi embassy or consulate once the visa is issued, upload a photo of the printed visa for the ticket, and bring originals certified by the Saudi embassy or cultural attache. That page looks like the older flow. The Telegram relay (#577, #1005) and the guide agree with each other and disagree with the IU page. Keep the guide, but add one line that original documents certified by the embassy or cultural attache are still expected on arrival, which does not conflict with an electronic visa.
4. **Residency for new students.** Guides state new students get a Visitor ID and no iqama. IU's Reception Unit page still lists first-time residency issuance and family residency IDs. Likely out of date or for earlier cohorts, but the University has not said so publicly. Related: guide `iqama-and-exit-reentry-fees` says "Students on a study visa pay nothing for their own iqama." This is true only for cohorts that get an iqama; say so in the sentence.
5. **SIM sentence.** `sim-internet-bank` says "All you need is your passport with a valid visa (or your iqama)." Two aggregator sources add the border number and a fingerprint at the counter. Low confidence, but the border number is the item students are told to keep (also used for Absher activation in the group chat file).
6. **Bank guide completeness.** `sim-internet-bank` lists a Saudi mobile number, Visitor ID and Nafath or Absher. The official SAMA rule also asks for a Saudi residential address, a home-country address and a purpose of visit, and ends services when the Visitor ID expires. The guide has no warning about freezing at expiry. Not a contradiction, a gap that can lead to a frozen card.
7. **Medical.** The Study in Saudi page lists a medical report and medical insurance for dependants as visa requirements; the Telegram relay (#1005) says medical is not enforced in practice. MESH currently says neither. If the guide is updated, say "the portal lists it; students have not been asked in practice; bring a simple letter."
8. **Deferral.** `defer-my-place` and the applying guide line "the University allows new students to defer for up to two years" is already hedged in the FAQ. The Telegram evidence (#536, #713, #221) still points against deferral being honoured, so the main guide sentence should carry the same hedge.
9. **Free housing.** `campus-life-islamic-university` says "Free housing is for unmarried students." This holds for earlier years; for the 2025 and 2026 intake the channel says new scholarship students may not get a room (#474, #1011). The earlier research already flagged this; I could not find any new contradiction beyond it.

## C. Proposed new articles, ranked

Ranking is by how often the topic appears across everything read so far (this file, the group chat file and the multilingual file) and by how costly a wrong answer is. I could not count independent posts across the whole web, so the order is a judgement, not a measured frequency.

1. **When your Visitor ID runs out: what happens to your bank card, SIM and apps**
   - Why: the SAMA rule freezes accounts at expiry; the group chat reports SIM cut-offs every 6 to 7 months; Absher activation fails for some. No guide ties these together.
   - Outline: what the Visitor ID is and who issues it; the expiry date and where to see it; what freezes (bank, mada card, SIM, Nafath); renewing before it lapses; who to contact at the University (Tawasul, Educational Visa Unit, helpdesk); a checklist for the week before expiry; what to do if a card is frozen abroad. Note the date of every rule.
2. **Who to ask at the University for what (office map for new students)**
   - Evidence: IU Reception Unit and Travel Management pages, Tawasul, the Educational Visa Unit and the helpdesk in the group chat, plus the Deanship of Admission phone and email on iu.edu.sa.
   - Outline: tickets and holiday tickets; passport validity; exit and return; first Visitor ID; lost passport; family; which portal (Tawasul, helpdesk, Minhati, academic system); what to write in a request; reply times students report.
3. **Renting on your own: contract terms, payment cycles and subletting**
   - Evidence: #1013, #1012, #1011, group chat rent figures. The existing guide covers Ejar; this adds up-front payment norms, cash discount for paying a year, subletting, taking over from a leaver, repairs.
   - Outline: official versus informal; Ejar through an accredited agent; unfurnished versus furnished; what to ask the landlord; deposit and instalments; what happens to furniture when you leave; red flags. Prices as ranges with dates.
4. **A realistic first-year budget for a married student**
   - Evidence: stipend 840 SAR with no rise for family, rent 18,000 SAR a year with half up front, school fees, the shared budget sheet in #503, fundraising appeal. Keep impartial and free of appeals for money.
   - Outline: income (stipend, tutoring); fixed costs (rent, utilities, internet, mobile); family costs (school, insurance); one-off costs (furnishing, deposits, flights); what students from different regions report spending; where to cut.
5. **Hajj as a student: resident packages, funded places and Visitor ID questions**
   - Evidence: #507, Nusuk residency rules; the group chat lists Hajj registration among Visitor ID problems.
   - Outline: resident-priced packages in 2025; how the University and charities fund a few final-year students; Nusuk registration and what counts as residence; what to ask the University; calendar. Mark eligibility of Visitor ID holders as unconfirmed.
6. **After final acceptance: what the University page says and what students report**
   - Evidence: the IU eight-step page versus Telegram relay.
   - Outline: provisional letter; passport details and departure point; ticket request; embassy or no embassy; what to carry (certified originals, health letter, photos); arrival date; first week.
7. **Schools and Qur'an classes for children: what exists and what to ask**
   - Evidence: only directory and aggregator material; thin. Needs parent testimony first.
   - Outline: kinds of school (national, international, Qur'an focused); fee ranges; application timing and waiting lists; what a visit or student visa allows; Qur'an and Arabic classes for children and sisters; how to ask a school. Publish only after the admins verify.
8. **From the online Arabic year to Madinah: what you must re-apply for**
   - Evidence: #1020, #1022, #1038, #1037.
   - Outline: who is offered the online year; what priority means; why a new Study in Saudi application is still needed; the conditional Arabic agreement; no stipend while online.
