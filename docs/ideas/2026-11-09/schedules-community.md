# Schedules, events, knowledge bases and community: ideas for MESH

Area: duroos schedule, updates, community contributions with moderation, FAQ and help centre, the assistant, digests, personalisation without accounts, accounts.

Studied: Luma, Meetup, Eventbrite, Google and Apple Calendar subscriptions, Citymapper, Transit, Moovit, university timetable apps, Mindbody, FotMob, Duolingo, Stack Overflow, Discourse, Notion and Intercom help centres, Wikipedia, Product Hunt, Hacker News, WhatsApp Channels, Telegram channels, Substack, Discord.

Checked against the repo (read only): push alerts already follow topics in localStorage (`mesh-push-topics`, `AlertsButton.tsx`); .ics feeds exist per feed name (`CalendarButton.tsx`); the manifest already has shortcuts; `public/sw.js` handles push but caches nothing for offline use; there are no favourites, no "my duroos", no RSS for updates and no public suggestion form yet.

Tag key: type | effort S/M/L | impact high/medium/low | inspired by

---

## 1. Now and next (transit style)

1. **A "Now and next" strip at the top of /duroos and home.** Why: Transit opens straight onto the next departures nearby, with no menus. How: one row with what is running now (with a progress bar), what starts next (big countdown) and the one after. It is driven by the prayer times you already compute, with today's overrides applied. | UX | S | high | Transit, Citymapper
2. **Large countdown numerals with a smaller clock time beneath.** Why: Transit puts the minutes left in a big number and the clock time beside it, so you can read it at arm's length while walking. How: "12 min" in bold MESH green, with "after Maghrib, about 6:04 pm" underneath. Above 60 min, switch to the clock time only. | aesthetic | S | medium | Transit
3. **A running state with elapsed time.** Why: FotMob's live minute ("67'") and progress bar tell you at once whether it is worth going in late. How: a "Live, 25 min in" pill with a thin gold bar that fills towards the usual end time. Admin sets a typical length per lesson (default 45 min). | feature | S | high | FotMob
4. **One set of states shared by the card, the lesson page, the feed and push.** Why: FotMob uses the same states everywhere (Upcoming, Live, FT, Postponed, Cancelled), so users learn them once. How: Upcoming, Starting soon (15 min), Live, Ended, Cancelled today, Moved (new time or place), Paused until a date, Resumes today. Extend `StatusTag.tsx`; never show a guessed state. | UX | M | high | FotMob, Transit
5. **A "Confirmed today" mark for duroos the watcher saw announced today.** Why: Transit shows live times with a pulsing icon and schedule-only times in grey. MESH has a real equivalent: a Telegram post from today confirming the dars. How: a small pulsing green dot labelled "Confirmed today" on cards with a same-day confirmation. Other cards show the plain weekly time, with no doubt wording. | trust | M | high | Transit
6. **A service status banner: "All duroos running as normal today."** Why: Citymapper's "Good service" line status answers the question most people have before they open anything else. How: a single line at the top of /duroos. It is green when there are no changes; when there are changes, gold with "2 changes today" linking to the changes feed. | UX | S | high | Citymapper, Transit
7. **A date pill strip (Yesterday, Today, Tomorrow, then weekdays with Hijri dates).** Why: FotMob and Mindbody both use a horizontal date scroller, which beats a dropdown on a phone. How: pills showing the weekday and Hijri day. Swiping changes the day; Today is pinned. Add an "Ended" divider so earlier duroos fall below the fold. | UX | S | medium | FotMob, Mindbody
8. **"Tonight", "Tomorrow morning" and "This Jumu'ah" quick views.** Why: Eventbrite's "Today, This weekend" chips match how people actually plan. How: chips that set the date and prayer range, for example Tonight = Maghrib and Isha today. Each view gets a shareable URL like `/duroos?when=tonight`. | UX | S | medium | Eventbrite, Meetup
9. **A low-data text view of today's duroos.** Why: Hacker News pages load instantly on any connection, and signal inside the Haram is poor at busy times. How: `/duroos/today/lite` is plain HTML under 15 KB with no images, the same data and an Arabic twin. Link it from the footer and the offline page. | performance | S | medium | Hacker News
10. **Today's and this week's schedule available offline.** Why: Transit keeps schedules usable without a signal, and people check MESH inside the masjid. How: add a fetch handler to `sw.js` that caches the duroos JSON and the /duroos shell, stale-while-revalidate. Show "Saved for offline, updated 10 min ago" when offline. | performance | M | high | Transit, Citymapper

## 2. Live changes and the cancellations feed

11. **A dedicated changes feed at `/duroos/changes`.** Why: Transit and Citymapper keep disruptions in one place, separate from the timetable. How: today and the next 7 days of cancellations, moves, pauses, new books and new duroos. Filter by masjid, shaykh or type; each item links to its lesson. Arabic twin. | feature | M | high | Citymapper, Telegram
12. **Cancelled cards stay visible, struck through, with a short reason and a resume date.** Why: FotMob keeps a postponed match in the list with its new date, and Transit strikes through cancelled trips; quietly removing the card makes people think they misremembered. How: strike through the time, add a "Cancelled today: Shaykh travelling. Resumes Sunday" line, and keep the card until the original end time. | UX | S | high | FotMob, Transit
13. **A public change history on each lesson page.** Why: Wikipedia's history makes a page trustworthy, and it doubles as useful content. How: a short timeline ("Started Bulugh al-Maram, 2 Rabi' al-Awwal 1448"; "Moved from Asr to Maghrib, 5 Jumada al-Ula") built from overrides and admin activity. It never names a source. | trust | M | medium | Wikipedia
14. **Cancellations written into the .ics feeds properly.** Why: a calendar subscription that still shows a cancelled dars is worse than no subscription. How: `STATUS:CANCELLED` plus a "Cancelled: " title prefix on that occurrence (RECURRENCE-ID or EXDATE), and moved occurrences sent as overridden instances. Calendar apps then update on their own. | function | M | high | Google Calendar, Apple Calendar
15. **Quiet hours for push, with urgent changes as the only exception.** Why: Telegram's silent posts and FotMob's per-event toggles stop alert fatigue. How: no push between Isha plus 2 h and Fajr except for a same-day cancellation of a dars the person follows. Night-time items roll into a morning summary. | function | S | medium | Telegram, FotMob
16. **Per-follow alert toggles.** Why: FotMob lets you pick which events to hear about per team (lineups, kick-off, goals). How: in the follow sheet for a shaykh or lesson, offer "Starts in 15 min", "Cancelled or moved" and "New book or new dars", saved in the existing topics array (for example `shaykh:x:cancel`). | feature | M | high | FotMob
17. **"Notify me when it starts" for announced future series.** Why: Product Hunt's "Notify me" on upcoming launches turns curiosity into a return visit. How: when a new book or a series after Ramadan is announced, the lesson page shows "Starts 1 Shawwal" with a follow button that pushes once on the first day. | feature | S | medium | Product Hunt, Luma

## 3. Filters, favourites and "My duroos" (no account)

18. **A star on every dars card, saved in localStorage.** Why: every timetable app has favourites; it is the lowest-effort personalisation. How: a `mesh-fav-lessons` array, a star on DarsCard, the lesson page and the day arc popover. Unlike push alerts, it needs no permission. | feature | S | high | Transit, Mindbody, FotMob
19. **A "My duroos" tab that comes first once you have a favourite.** Why: FotMob's "Following" tab turns a big fixture list into your own. How: /duroos opens on "My duroos" (today's favourites, then the rest of the week) whenever favourites exist, with "All duroos" one tap away. On the home page, the now and next strip prefers favourites. | UX | M | high | FotMob, Discord
20. **Remembered filters, shown as chips.** Why: Mindbody and Eventbrite keep filters (instructor, location, time of day) as removable chips. How: masjid, prayer, language, audience (brothers, sisters, both) and level, encoded in the URL that already exists (`?prayer=&masjid=`) and remembered locally. A "Clear" chip resets them. | UX | S | medium | Mindbody, Eventbrite
21. **Language and level filters on duroos.** Why: university timetable apps filter by course level, and a new Ma'had student needs to know what they can follow. How: add `language` (Arabic, English, both) and `level` (beginner, intermediate, advanced) fields, which audit item 19 already asks for, and show them as small tags. | content | M | high | Uni timetable apps, Mindbody
22. **A personal "My duroos" calendar feed with no account.** Why: subscription calendars are the strongest retention tool on Luma and Google Calendar, and a per-person feed beats twenty single feeds. How: `/api/v1/calendar/my.ics?l=<short code>`, where the code is a server-stored, opaque list of lesson IDs created when the person taps "Subscribe to my duroos". There is no identity, only the list. | feature | M | high | Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, Luma
23. **Move favourites to another device with a link or QR code.** Why: personalisation without accounts breaks when someone changes phones. Some apps solve this with export codes. How: "Move to another device" shows a QR code of a URL carrying favourites, follows, persona and language (base64 in the hash, so the server never sees it). Opening it on the other phone imports everything. | function | S | medium | Duolingo (QR login), Signal (transfer)
24. **A "Personalise MESH" sheet with persona onboarding.** Why: Discord onboarding asks a few questions and then shows only the relevant channels. How: one optional sheet asking "I am a: Kulliyah student, Ma'had student, sister, family, planning to come, visiting", stored locally. It reorders home blocks, for example visa and arrival guides first for "planning to come" and the Ma'had hub for Ma'had students. | UX | M | high | Discord, Notion
25. **A "Forget this device" button.** Why: trust grows when people can see and clear what is stored. How: on /more, list what MESH keeps on this phone (favourites, follows, ticked steps, language) with one button to clear it and unsubscribe from push. | trust | S | medium | Discord, Duolingo settings

## 4. Reminders and calendar

26. **One "Add to calendar" sheet with every target.** Why: Luma's add-to-calendar covers Google, Apple, Outlook and a download in one tap. How: on each lesson, offer "This dars only", "Every week (subscribe)" and "This shaykh", with Google, Apple (webcal) and a .ics download. The Android notes from `CalendarButton.tsx` stay. | UX | S | medium | Luma, Eventbrite
27. **Alarms built into feeds.** Why: Apple Calendar honours VALARM on subscriptions, so the reminder works with no push permission. How: add a VALARM 20 min before each occurrence in personal and shaykh feeds; the "My duroos" feed URL has a reminder setting (`&remind=10|20|30`). | function | S | medium | Apple Calendar
28. **"Leave by" reminders based on walking time.** Why: Citymapper's "Leave in 6 min" is the reminder that actually gets you there. How: when the person has saved Home (see 31), a favourite's reminder reads "Leave by 5:48 pm for after Maghrib at the Haram (14 min walk)". It is computed on the device, and the push fires at the leave time if they opt in. | feature | M | high | Citymapper
29. **A Fajr morning brief by push.** Why: Telegram channels and Substack daily editions bundle the day into one message instead of many pings. How: an optional daily push after Fajr, such as "Today: 6 duroos, 1 change. Your duroos: Tafsir after Asr, Sahih Muslim after Maghrib". The `daily` topic already exists; personalise it with the topic list. | feature | S | high | Telegram, Substack
30. **Ramadan, Hajj and exam season views.** Why: university timetable apps switch between term timetables, and Madinah's dars calendar changes completely in Ramadan. How: a season field on overrides (Ramadan, Hajj break, university exams) and a banner "Ramadan timetable in effect until 1 Shawwal" that switches the schedule and feeds together. | feature | M | high | Uni timetable apps, Google Calendar

## 5. Travel time, places and the map

31. **Save Home on this device.** Why: Citymapper's saved Home and Work power every "get me there" feature. How: one tap uses device location once and stores rounded coordinates locally (never sent). Cards then show "14 min walk", with a "By car or taxi" option. | feature | M | high | Citymapper
32. **Gate and spot directions inside the Haram.** Why: in the Prophet's Masjid, "where" means which gate and which part of the masjid, which no map app gives you. How: a location field per lesson ("Enter by Gate 21, in the old masjid near the Rawdah side"), shown with a photo or a small diagram. Directions open Google Maps to the right gate's coordinates rather than the whole Haram. | content | M | high | Citymapper (station exits), Transit
33. **A schematic map of the Haram showing today's duroos.** Why: Citymapper's station exit maps and Moovit's stop maps beat a satellite view in big complexes. How: an SVG outline of an-Nabawi with numbered gates and gold diamonds where today's duroos are. Tapping a diamond opens the card; there are separate layers for brothers' and sisters' areas. | aesthetic | L | high | Citymapper, Moovit
34. **A map and list toggle across all masjids.** Why: Eventbrite and Meetup let you switch between list and map; families in outer districts want what is near them. How: a map of the Haram, Quba and other masjids with a count of today's duroos as a pin badge, and a list sorted by distance from saved Home. Static tiles so no API key is needed. | feature | M | medium | Eventbrite, Meetup, Moovit
35. **"Next dars near me now".** Why: Transit's nearby view is the whole app for many users. How: a button using device location once that shows the nearest masjids with their next dars and walking time. | feature | S | medium | Transit, Moovit

## 6. Recurring rules and the data model (admin facing)

36. **Store schedules as recurrence rules with exceptions.** Why: Google Calendar's RRULE, EXDATE and RDATE model handles "Saturday to Wednesday except during exams" without one-off hacks. How: a weekday set, prayer anchor, offset minutes, start and end dates, and a season list per lesson. Overrides become exceptions. This also produces the .ics files directly. | operations | L | high | Google Calendar
37. **Edit "this occurrence, following ones, or all" in admin.** Why: Google Calendar's three-way edit prevents the most common scheduling error. How: when an editor changes a time in admin, ask "Only on 12 Jumada al-Ula / From then on / Always" and write an override, a new rule version, or an edit to the base rule. | operations | M | high | Google Calendar
38. **Per-masjid offsets for iqamah and prayer length.** Why: "After Maghrib" is not the adhan time; timetable apps that show exact times win trust. How: admin sets the iqamah offset and typical prayer length per masjid and prayer (seasonal for Isha in Ramadan), and the countdown targets the real start. This fits with plan item 4 (`timeKnown`). | function | M | high | Uni timetable apps, Transit
39. **Book progress for each dars.** Why: FotMob's season view and a university course's syllabus progress both give a feeling of moving forward. How: an optional "Now at: Kitab as-Salah, Bab Sifat as-Salah" field updated by admin or by the watcher, shown on the lesson page with the book's place in its sequence and a link to the library reader. | content | M | medium | FotMob, uni course apps

## 7. Attendance and gentle habit building (private only)

40. **A private "I attended" check-in.** Why: Duolingo's streak is the best-known habit device. In this context, though, publicity invites showing off, so keep it private to the device. How: after a favourite's end time, the card shows "Did you attend?", one tap, stored locally. There are no public counts or leaderboards, ever. | feature | S | medium | Duolingo
41. **A weekly goal, not a daily streak.** Why: duroos are not daily for most people, and Duolingo's own research says flexibility keeps learners going. How: "Your goal: 3 duroos a week", with a week ring that fills in gold. | UX | S | medium | Duolingo
42. **Automatic pauses for travel, exams and cancellations.** Why: Duolingo's streak freeze shows that slack keeps people engaged, and losing a streak to a cancelled dars feels unjust. How: cancelled duroos never count against the week, and a "Travelling" or "Exams" pause (up to 2 weeks) holds the count. | UX | S | medium | Duolingo (streak freeze)
43. **A calm year view of attendance.** Why: the contribution heatmap gives a long view without daily pressure. How: a 12-month grid of diamonds (the lattice motif) filled gold on days attended, by Hijri month, on "My duroos". | aesthetic | S | low | Duolingo, GitHub
44. **Personal notes per dars.** Why: Notion-style notes next to a timetable entry are what students already do on paper. How: a notes field on the lesson page in "My duroos" for benefits and page numbers, stored locally and exportable as text. | feature | M | medium | Notion, uni timetable apps
45. **An "In the dars" view.** Why: Transit's GO mode strips the app down to the trip you are on. How: while a favourite is live, its card offers "Open dars view": the book in the library reader at the current chapter, the livestream link, your notes, and what is on next after this prayer. | feature | M | medium | Transit (GO), FotMob match centre

## 8. Updates, announcements and channels

46. **"New since your last visit".** Why: Discourse draws a line at your last visit, which is the fastest way to catch up. How: store the last-seen timestamp locally. On `/duroos/updates` and the home updates block, draw a gold line at it and show a dot on the Updates nav item when something is new. | UX | S | high | Discourse
47. **A type tag on every update, with filters.** Why: Telegram channels use hashtags (#cancelled, #exams) so readers can filter a busy feed. How: types Schedule change, University, Visa and iqama, Haram, Community, shown as tags with one-tap filters and a URL per filter. | UX | S | medium | Telegram
48. **A pinned post and an expiry on every update.** Why: Telegram's pinned message and WhatsApp Channel pins keep the one critical notice on top. Stale notices damage trust. How: one pinned update at the top of the feed, and a required "show until" date on each update so the board clears itself. This ties into `docs/EXPIRING.md`. | operations | S | high | Telegram, WhatsApp Channels
49. **A MESH WhatsApp Channel mirrored from Updates.** Why: the audience lives in WhatsApp, and Channels are one-way, have no replies and do not expose members' numbers. How: Admin's "Publish update" also produces a WhatsApp-formatted text (bold title, two lines, link) to paste into the Channel, plus a daily "Today's duroos" image (see 50). | community | S | high | WhatsApp Channels
50. **A share image for every dars, shaykh and day.** Why: Luma's event images make links look worth tapping in chats. How: `opengraph-image.tsx` per lesson ("Tonight after Isha, Sahih Muslim, Masjid an-Nabawi") in green, gold and lattice, with a daily "Today at the Haram" card for groups. | aesthetic | M | high | Luma, WhatsApp
51. **An RSS or Atom feed for updates and changes.** Why: Substack and every news site offer feeds. Feeds also power Telegram and WhatsApp mirror bots and a future native app cheaply. How: `/duroos/updates/feed.xml` and `/duroos/changes/feed.xml`, with Arabic twins. | function | S | medium | Substack, Telegram
52. **Reactions on updates instead of comments.** Why: WhatsApp Channels allow emoji reactions with no replies, which gives feedback with no moderation load. How: three reactions (helpful, JazakAllah khair, question), counted anonymously in the track endpoint with no IP stored. "Question" opens the WhatsApp admin link prefilled with the update title. | community | S | low | WhatsApp Channels

## 9. Newsletters and digests

53. **"The week ahead in Madinah", every Saturday.** Why: Substack shows that one dependable weekly issue beats many alerts. How: generated from data with a short editor's note: new duroos, changes, university key dates, updated guides, Islamic dates and one new FAQ. It goes out by push, the WhatsApp Channel and a web archive page per issue, which also helps SEO. | content | M | high | Substack, Product Hunt (daily digest)
54. **An optional email digest, with email as the only thing stored.** Why: Substack is email first, and email reaches families abroad who are planning to come. How: one field with double opt-in and a one-click unsubscribe. Use the same issue as 53. There are no accounts; the address is the subscription. Add this once the privacy page exists near launch. | community | M | medium | Substack
55. **"Before you arrive", a timed series for people planning to come.** Why: Substack's welcome sequence and Duolingo's onboarding drip spread a big task into steps. How: a 6 to 8 week series (visa, documents, what to pack, first week, Visitor ID, SIM, bank), each linking to its guide. Ticked steps from guides show as done. | content | M | high | Substack, Duolingo

## 10. Community contributions and moderation

56. **"Suggest an edit" on every dars, shaykh, guide section and FAQ.** Why: Wikipedia and Google Maps prove that small, structured corrections keep content fresh. How: a sheet with fields that fit the item (time, prayer, masjid, days, book, "no longer running") and a free-text note. It goes into the admin "Needs you" queue as a proposed diff with one-tap Accept, which writes the change, logs it and supports Undo. | community | M | high | Wikipedia, Google Maps, Stack Overflow (suggested edits)
57. **A one-tap "Did not happen today?" on live and ended cards.** Why: Moovit's community reports catch disruptions that agencies miss. How: one tap, deduplicated per device per occurrence. Three reports in an hour raise an admin alert and tell the watcher to re-check Telegram. Nothing is shown publicly until an editor confirms. | community | S | high | Moovit
58. **A "Submit a dars" form for regular attenders.** Why: Meetup grows because organisers add events themselves. How: a structured form (shaykh, book, masjid and spot, after which prayer, days via a weekday picker, language, audience, Telegram link). The duplicate check from 61 runs while it is filled in. It goes to admin review, then is published with the same shape as admin-created duroos. | community | M | high | Meetup, Luma (submit to calendar)
59. **"Is this still right?" at the end of each guide section.** Why: Intercom and Notion help centres ask for feedback at the point of reading, and stale facts are MESH's main known risk. How: Yes or "Something changed", where the second opens a short note. Counts appear in admin next to the section's last-verified date, and sections with recent "changed" votes rise to the top of the editor's list. | trust | S | high | Intercom, Wikipedia
60. **A reference code so contributors can follow their suggestion.** Why: Stack Overflow shows the status of a suggested edit, which builds trust that suggestions are read. How: after sending, show "Reference M-4821" and a status page (`/suggest/M-4821`: received, accepted, not needed) with no personal data, saved locally under "My suggestions". | community | S | medium | Stack Overflow
61. **Show similar items while someone is typing.** Why: Stack Overflow and Discourse show possible duplicates as you write, which cuts duplicate reports. How: use the existing search index while someone writes a suggestion, dars submission or Ask question. "This may already be here" lists matching duroos, FAQs and guides. | UX | S | high | Stack Overflow, Discourse
62. **Trusted contributor links, not accounts.** Why: Discourse trust levels give regular helpers more power without opening the door to everyone. How: Admin issues a personal contributor link (a signed token kept on the device) to known regulars. Their reports skip the triage tier and their "did not happen" counts as three, and the link can be revoked in Admin > Admins. | operations | M | medium | Discourse
63. **AI triage and anti-spam before anything reaches an editor.** Why: Discourse's flag reasons and Stack Overflow's review queues keep moderators focused. How: a honeypot field, a per-device rate limit, and the existing Gemini watcher that classifies each submission (correction, new dars, spam, duplicate) and attaches the matching Telegram post when it finds one. Editors always make the final decision. | operations | M | medium | Discourse, Stack Overflow
64. **A quiet thank-you on the contributor's device.** Why: Wikipedia's "thanks" button motivates people without public rankings. How: when a suggestion is accepted, the status page shows "Accepted. JazakAllahu khayran", and a push is sent if they follow it. There are no public leaderboards, which keeps things impartial and modest. | community | S | low | Wikipedia

## 11. FAQ and help centre

65. **Search first on Ask, with instant results.** Why: Intercom and Notion help centres open on one large search box with results as you type. How: put the search box at the top of /ask, scoped to FAQs and guides, with persona chips beneath. Typing shows answers before the category list. | UX | S | high | Intercom, Notion
66. **Short answer first, then details.** Why: Stack Overflow's accepted answer and a featured snippet give the answer in the first line. How: every FAQ opens with one or two bold sentences, followed by steps and links. The short answer is also what search, the assistant and share text use. | content | M | high | Stack Overflow, Intercom
67. **Three-face helpful vote with "What was missing?"** Why: Intercom's reactions get far more responses than yes or no plus a form. How: three faces under each answer, with the sad face opening one optional line. Counts by answer appear in admin; nothing public. | trust | S | high | Intercom
68. **A searches-with-no-answer report.** Why: help centres treat failed searches as the content backlog. How: log the normalised query text (no IP, no device ID) when site search, Ask or the assistant finds nothing, and show the top 20 weekly in owner analytics as "Questions MESH does not answer yet". | operations | S | high | Intercom, Algolia
69. **"Copy for WhatsApp" on every answer and guide step.** Why: in Madinah groups, people answer repeat questions by pasting links. Make the pasted text good. How: a button that copies the short answer in WhatsApp formatting (`*bold*`, line breaks) plus the deep link to that exact answer or heading. | community | S | high | WhatsApp, Stack Overflow (share link)
70. **A help button on every guide that searches first and then contacts Admin.** Why: the Intercom Messenger pattern answers from articles before handing over to a person. How: a floating "Need help?" button opens a sheet with three related answers for this page, then "Message Admin on WhatsApp" with a prefilled `wa.me` text naming the page and section. | UX | S | high | Intercom
71. **"Updated for 1448" labels on time-sensitive answers.** Why: Notion and Intercom show updated dates, and Wikipedia uses "as of" markers. How: a Hijri year badge on fees, dates and rules answers, set by the editor, with an `EXPIRING.md` reminder when the next year starts. This states currency with authority rather than doubt. | trust | S | medium | Wikipedia, Notion
72. **"Top questions this week" on /ask.** Why: Stack Overflow's hot questions and Intercom's popular articles point people to what is relevant now (exam season, iqama renewal season). How: the five most-viewed FAQs over 7 days from your own analytics, by persona when one is set. | content | S | medium | Stack Overflow, Intercom

## 12. The assistant

73. **Answers that include action cards.** Why: Intercom's Fin answers with article cards and buttons, not just text. How: when the assistant mentions a dars, show the DarsCard with star, add-to-calendar and directions buttons; for a guide, a guide card with "Start steps". The assistant already cites MESH pages, so render those citations as cards. | feature | M | high | Intercom (Fin)
74. **A live schedule tool for the assistant.** Why: "What is on after Isha tonight near Gate 21?" is the most natural question, and answers from static text go stale. How: give the assistant a read-only tool that returns today's resolved schedule with overrides, so it answers with the current state and the "Confirmed today" mark. | function | M | high | Transit, Intercom
75. **Prompt suggestions by persona and page.** Why: Notion AI and Intercom show starter prompts that fit the context. How: on a guide, show "What do I need for this?"; for the Ma'had persona, "Which book is level 2?". Show three chips; the empty state is never blank. | UX | S | medium | Notion, Intercom
76. **A smooth handover to Admin.** Why: Intercom passes the conversation to a person with context. How: when the assistant has no answer, or after a sad-face vote, show "Message Admin on WhatsApp" with a prefilled summary of the question (not the full transcript). The question is also logged in the no-answer report (68). | UX | S | high | Intercom
77. **"Ask about this page".** Why: Notion AI works on the page you are reading. How: a small entry on guides and lesson pages that opens the assistant scoped to that page first (citations from that page rank higher). | feature | S | medium | Notion

## 13. Accounts: verdict

78. **No accounts for now; device storage plus three opt-in channels.** Why: none of the above needs identity. Luma and Substack show that an email or a calendar subscription is enough of a relationship, and accounts would add a privacy page, password resets and moderation of profiles. How: localStorage for favourites, attendance, notes and persona; push topics and the short-code calendar feed for reminders; an optional email for the digest; contributor links for trusted helpers.
