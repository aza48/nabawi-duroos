# Ideas from student and expat settling-in sites

Area: websites that help international students and expats settle in a new city. Studied: university international offices (Oxford, Toronto CIE and Rotman, Melbourne, NUS OSA, IU Madinah and Al-Azhar portals as seen through applicant guides), Study in Saudi, Expatica, InterNations, Expat.com, Nomad List (nomads.com), Numbeo, Wise and Revolut guides, GOV.UK (travel advice, "Living in", step-by-step and smart answers), Citizens Advice, Settlement.org (First Days), Saudi portals (Absher, my.gov.sa, Muqeem, Ejar), Reddit community wikis, Wikivoyage, Lonely Planet, company relocation welcome guides and arrival checklist apps.

Already on the MESH roadmap (ROADMAP.md, "Feature brainstorm" and Plan 4): arrival checklist, leaving checklist, important numbers offline, cost-of-living guide, places directory, bus times, family section, newsletter, share cards, re-check reminders. Ideas below that touch these say how to go further than the roadmap line.

Tag key: type | effort (S, M, L) | impact (high, medium, low) | inspired by.

## Summary: the strongest ideas

1. **First 100 days timeline** anchored to the student's own arrival date: before you fly, arrival day, first 72 hours, week 1, month 1, semester 1, year 1, each step linked to an existing guide and ticked off on the device (A1, A2).
2. **"Who are you?" onboarding in four taps** (brother or sister, single or family, Ma'had, Kulliyah, postgrad, visitor, planning to come) that reorders the home page, guides and checklist for that path (B1, B2).
3. **Document wallet with expiry reminders** for passport, iqama, exit re-entry visa, insurance and each dependant, in Hijri and Gregorian, kept on the phone only, with push and .ics reminders at 90, 30 and 7 days (C1, C2, C3).
4. **Exit re-entry trip checker**: enter travel dates, see if the trip fits inside the iqama and passport validity and which visa type and fee applies (C4).
5. **GOV.UK style "smart answers"** for the questions people ask the WhatsApp group most: can I bring my family, can I work, do I need a new visa for this trip, which guide is mine (B4).
6. **Budget calculator by path** using MESH's own cost figures, with a home-currency converter and a one-off setup cost list (D1, D2, D4).
7. **Step-by-step journeys** (GOV.UK pattern) that chain guides in order, plus annotated screenshots of Absher, Nafath and university e-services with the exact Arabic button text beside the English (E1, E2, E4).
8. **Public "What changed" page and a one-line "Latest update" at the top of every guide**, with per-guide follow so a student is told when the iqama or exit re-entry guide changes (I1, I2, I3).
9. **Glossary of local terms** (iqama, muqeem, jawazat, ta'jeel, i'tithaar, mukafaa, zaer, Nafath, Ejar, sakan) auto-linked inside every guide, plus a situational phrasebook with show-the-clerk cards (G1, G2, G4).
10. **Printable and offline arrival pack** (checklist, numbers, glossary, map, Arabic cards) generated from live content, saved by the service worker and given out as a QR at orientation (K1, K2, K4).

## A. Arrival journey and timelines

- **A1. First 100 days timeline.** One page with stages: Before you come (3 months, 1 month, 1 week), Arrival day, First 72 hours, First week, First month, First semester, First year. Why: every strong international office (Rotman "first day, week and month", Settlement.org "First Days", Melbourne "New Student Checklist") frames settling as a sequence, not a pile of guides. How for MESH: a `/start` page built from existing guide slugs, each step a card with a tick saved on the device (reuse the guide tick-off code), a progress bar per stage, and one line saying what to do next. Goes beyond the roadmap's flat "arrival checklist". `feature | M | high | Rotman, Settlement.org, Melbourne`
- **A2. Arrival-date anchored due dates.** The student enters their arrival date (Gregorian or Hijri) and every timeline step shows a real "do by" date. Why: deadlines like Toronto's 30-day insurance enrolment are what people miss; in Madinah the medical exam, iqama issue and first stipend have similar windows. How for MESH: one date stored in localStorage, steps carry an offset in days, dates shown in both calendars with the existing Umm al-Qura helper. `function | S | high | U of T CIE, Settlement.org`
- **A3. First 72 hours card.** A single short card: at Prince Mohammad bin Abdulaziz airport, getting to the Jamiah or your housing, SIM on arrival, cash for the first days, first meal, nearest masjid and prayer times, who to message. Why: Melbourne and student blogs single out the first 48 hours; it is when people are tired, offline and anxious. How for MESH: one guide plus a home banner for anyone whose arrival date is within 3 days. `content | S | high | Melbourne, Birchridge 48 hours`
- **A4. Airport walkthrough with photos.** Plane to passport control to baggage to taxi or bus, with what officers ask, which papers to keep in hand and where to pray. Why: U of T staffs an airport welcome booth; MESH can give the same calm in photos. How for MESH: photo steps (taken by a volunteer on arrival), works offline, Arabic twin. `content | M | medium | U of T airport welcome, Wikivoyage "Get in"`
- **A5. Packing list by path and season.** What to bring (attested certificates, spare passport photos on white, prescriptions with generic names, winter layer for Madinah nights), what to buy here (thobes, bedding, books), what students regret. Why: every pre-arrival page has one and it is shared widely. How for MESH: a guide with tick-off items and filters for brother, sister, family with children. `content | S | medium | U of T pre-arrival, Expatica checklist`
- **A6. Pre-departure briefing pack.** A "Before you fly" page with a countdown to the University arrival window, documents to attest, cash needed until the first stipend, and what to do while waiting for the visa. Why: Melbourne runs a pre-departure briefing series; most MESH questions arrive before people land. How for MESH: page fed by the University calendar dates, linked from the planning path. `content | S | high | Melbourne pre-departure series`
- **A7. Campus orientation guide.** Where to collect the student card, the Ma'had registration hall, Deanship offices, clinic, cafeteria, gates for sisters, buses, with a simple campus map. Why: Melbourne's "Stop 1" and orientation pages answer "where do I go" in one place. How for MESH: one guide plus a static SVG map in MESH colours (this is item 0h "campus practicals" in HANDOFF). `content | M | high | Melbourne Stop 1, NUS OSA`
- **A8. "What matters now" seasonal banner.** The home page shows the 2 or 3 tasks for the current phase: registration window, exam period, summer break exit re-entry, Ramadan timings, Hajj registration. Why: university portals change their front page by term; MESH already has the calendar data. How for MESH: map calendar ranges to guide slugs in one config file; show under the hero. `UX | S | high | university portals, GOV.UK "latest"`

## B. Personalised paths and onboarding

- **B1. "Who are you?" onboarding in four taps.** Brother or sister; single or with family; Ma'had, Kulliyah, postgraduate, self-funded, visitor, planning to come; arrived or not yet. Why: Settlement.org and university sites split content by newcomer type; one-size lists bury the relevant 20 percent. How for MESH: a skippable sheet on first visit, answers in localStorage, used to reorder home cards, filter the timeline and pick the default library level. `UX | M | high | Settlement.org, Melbourne by student type`
- **B2. Path landing pages.** `/start/brother-mahad`, `/start/sister`, `/start/family`, `/start/postgraduate`, `/start/visitor`, `/start/applying`, each a curated, ordered list of guides and FAQs. Why: shareable links for the WhatsApp group ("new sister? start here") and strong search entry points. How for MESH: static pages from a config of slugs, English and Arabic twins. `SEO | S | high | U of T SGS international portal`
- **B3. Family path.** Dependants' visa and istiqdam deposit, iqama for wife and children, schools, sisters' study options, women's clinics, Haram women's sections, children's activities. Why: Expatica and company relocation guides always have a partner and children track; families have the most paperwork. How for MESH: path page plus a family section in the document wallet (C5). `content | M | high | Expatica family, company relocation guides`
- **B4. Smart answers for common decisions.** Short question flows with a definite outcome page: "Can I bring my family?", "Can I work on a student iqama?", "Do I need an exit re-entry for this trip?", "Can I defer?", "Which Arabic option suits me?". Why: GOV.UK smart answers and Citizens Advice checkers turn long rules into one clear answer; these are the most repeated WhatsApp questions. How for MESH: a small JSON decision tree engine, each outcome links to the guide; answers written with authority, no rulings. `feature | M | high | GOV.UK smart answers, Citizens Advice`
- **B5. Visitor path.** For people on Umrah or visit visas who want to attend duroos: what is possible on that visa, English-friendly duroos, Haram etiquette, how long they can stay. Why: Wikivoyage writes for the traveller; MESH visitors are a real audience the student-centred pages miss. How for MESH: path page linking duroos filters and the Visitor ID guide (HANDOFF 0h). `content | S | medium | Wikivoyage`
- **B6. Applicant path.** Admission cycle timeline, Study in Saudi portal walkthrough, waiting for results, attestation, what to study while waiting. Why: Study in Saudi publishes a user manual because its portal confuses applicants. How for MESH: path page plus annotated portal screenshots (E2). `content | M | high | Study in Saudi manual, THE counsellor guide`
- **B7. "My Madinah" dashboard.** One device-only page: checklist progress, next document due, duroos followed, saved guides, the current phase. Why: my.unimelb and Absher dashboards show "your" state at a glance. How for MESH: reads the same localStorage keys; no account, no server storage, so no privacy page needed yet. `feature | M | medium | my.unimelb, Absher dashboard`

## C. Documents, deadlines and reminders

- **C1. Document wallet.** Enter expiry dates for passport, iqama, exit re-entry visa, health insurance, student card, driving licence; see days left with green, gold and grey states. Why: Absher and Muqeem show expiry but nobody is reminded in English; late iqama renewal is fined and repeats escalate. How for MESH: on-device only, dates only (no numbers or scans), Hijri and Gregorian side by side. `feature | M | high | Absher, Muqeem`
- **C2. Expiry reminders by push and calendar.** Alerts at 90, 30 and 7 days, or an .ics file made in the browser with the reminders inside. Why: expat guides tell people to "set a calendar reminder 90 days before expiry"; MESH can do it for them. How for MESH: reuse the existing push service with a scheduled local payload, or generate .ics client side so nothing leaves the phone. `function | M | high | Absher SMS, expat guides`
- **C3. Hijri and Gregorian converter inline.** Wherever a date is shown or entered, a tap flips it. Why: Absher prints expiry in Hijri; Western students think in Gregorian. How for MESH: the Umm al-Qura helper already exists; add a small date chip component. `UX | S | medium | Absher, Muqeem`
- **C4. Exit re-entry trip checker.** Enter leave and return dates; the page says single or multiple, the fee band, whether the return falls after iqama or passport expiry, and what to do first. Why: commercial Saudi sites run exit re-entry calculators because people get this wrong; fees seen in Nov 2026: 200 SAR single for 2 months, 500 SAR multiple for 3 months, extra months extra (confirm before publishing). How for MESH: pure client function with tests, fee table in one config file listed in EXPIRING.md for review. `function | M | high | Saudi exit re-entry calculators, Muqeem`
- **C5. Family document rows.** One row per dependant for passport and iqama dates. Why: Absher's "Family Members" view is the model; sponsors renew dependants separately. How for MESH: extends C1. `feature | S | medium | Absher Family Members`
- **C6. "Bring these" document boxes.** At the top of each procedure guide: which originals and copies to carry (passport, iqama or Visitor ID, enrolment letter, Absher login, photos). Why: U of T and Settlement.org list documents to carry for each office; a wasted trip costs a day. How for MESH: a `documents` field in guide frontmatter rendered as a tickable box. `content | S | high | U of T CIE, Settlement.org`
- **C7. Attestation tracker for applicants and graduates.** Steps to attest certificates at home and at the Saudi embassy, and at the end of study for graduation papers. Why: Study in Saudi requirements and graduate offices both hinge on attestation; it is slow and order matters. How for MESH: a step-by-step journey (E1) with country notes (N3). `content | M | medium | Study in Saudi requirements`

## D. Money, cost of living and budgets

- **D1. Budget calculator by path.** Inputs: single in sakan, married off campus, rent band, children, transport mode, eating out. Output: a monthly range and a breakdown. Why: Numbeo and NUS cost tables are the most visited pages on their sites; MESH already has a cost guide (2,900 to 6,700 SAR). How for MESH: client-side calculator reading the cost guide's own figures so one edit updates both; saved on device. `feature | M | high | Numbeo calculator, NUS cost table`
- **D2. Home currency view.** Pick GBP, USD, CAD, EUR, NGN and so on once; every SAR figure on the site shows the home amount on tap. Why: Wise and Numbeo comparisons answer "is that a lot?". How for MESH: a free FX rate fetched daily and cached in the API, chip component on prices. `function | S | medium | Wise converter, Numbeo comparison`
- **D3. Everyday price list.** Bread, chicken, rice, a taxi to the Haram, a SIM bundle, a barber, rent bands by district, each with the month it was checked. Why: Nomad List and Numbeo win trust by showing many concrete prices with dates. How for MESH: a table in the cost guide; students send prices through a WhatsApp prefilled message, admins update. No shop names, so it stays impartial. `content | S | high | Numbeo, Nomad List`
- **D4. One-off setup costs.** Deposit, furniture, mattress and fridge, Ejar fee, SIM, iqama for dependants, istiqdam deposit, school fees. Why: Expatica budgeting pages separate setup from monthly; families underestimate setup. How for MESH: a second tab in the calculator. `content | S | medium | Expatica`
- **D5. First-month cash plan.** How much cash to bring, when the bank account is possible, when the first stipend lands. Why: NUS tells students to carry enough until a bank account opens; "stipend timing" is on MESH's content list. How for MESH: a section in the pre-departure pack (A6) and a stipend row in the calendar. `content | S | high | NUS OSA, Melbourne`
- **D6. Sending and receiving money, neutral.** Ways to move money (bank transfer, exchange houses, app transfers), what each needs (iqama, Absher, Nafath), limits and what fees to compare. Why: Wise and Revolut guides are useful because they explain the rails, not because of the brand. How for MESH: a guide that names methods, never one provider. `content | S | medium | Wise and Revolut guides`
- **D7. Stipend and payday calendar.** Expected stipend dates and the arrival reward shown in the University calendar, with countdowns. Why: university finance calendars; "when does the mukafaa come" is a constant question. How for MESH: rows in the existing calendar data. `content | S | high | university finance calendars`

## E. Procedures and step-by-step help

- **E1. Step-by-step journeys.** Numbered vertical journeys that chain guides: "Bring your family to Madinah", "From acceptance to first lesson", "Leave Saudi at graduation". Each member guide shows "Part of: ...". Why: GOV.UK step-by-step navigation fixed the problem of tasks spread across many pages. How for MESH: a `journeys` config of ordered slugs and a side panel on guide pages (fits the existing related-guides panel at 1280 px). `UX | M | high | GOV.UK step by step`
- **E2. Annotated screenshots for portals.** Absher, Nafath, Study in Saudi, University e-services, Ejar, Sehhaty: numbered screenshots with the tap target circled. Why: the Study in Saudi manual is a screenshot PDF because text alone fails. How for MESH: images in the guide body with captions; redact personal data; mark each with the month it was taken. `content | M | high | Study in Saudi manual`
- **E3. Summary box on every procedure.** Time it takes, cost, online or in person, where, documents. Why: GOV.UK service start pages answer these four before step one. How for MESH: frontmatter fields rendered as a card at the top of the guide. `UX | S | high | GOV.UK start pages`
- **E4. Arabic button labels beside steps.** "Tap Services, then Passports" shown with the exact Arabic text the student will see. Why: most portals are Arabic first; English guides fail at the last click. How for MESH: an inline `ar` label component in guide markdown. `content | S | high | Study in Saudi manual, bilingual portals`
- **E5. "If it goes wrong" section.** Common error messages and refusals with the fix: Absher error texts translated, iqama not yet issued, bank refusal on Visitor ID. Why: Citizens Advice and Reddit wikis are trusted for the failure path official pages skip. How for MESH: a standard last section in procedure guides. `content | S | medium | Citizens Advice, Reddit wikis`
- **E6. Office visit cards.** Jawazat, University deanships, hospital registration: hours, women's entrance, take a number, dress, what to say. Why: Settlement.org "Services Near Me" and Wikivoyage listings tell you what happens at the door. How for MESH: a short card component reused across guides, with a map link (existing `maps.ts`). `content | M | medium | Settlement.org, Wikivoyage`

Why: Settlement.org and Wikivoyage put services on a map; newcomers think in places. How for MESH: one static map page listing public institutions only, so no business is featured; pins open Google Maps. `feature | M | medium | Settlement.org, Wikivoyage`
- **F2. Neighbourhood guide for housing.** Districts near the Jamiah and the Haram: rent band, bus time to the Haram, families or singles, sisters' study centres nearby. Why: Nomad List neighbourhoods and Expat.com "choosing where to live" are top pages. How for MESH: a table in the housing guide with a small district map; no agencies named. `content | M | high | Nomad List, Expat.com`
- **F3. Route cards.** Jamiah to the Haram, Jamiah to Quba, Haram to the airport, Madinah to Makkah by Haramain train: options, fare, time, last service. Why: Wikivoyage "Get around" is the most used city section. How for MESH: extends the roadmap bus item with printable cards. `content | S | high | Wikivoyage`
- **F4. "Near me" for essentials.** On the essentials map, show the nearest masjid with a dars tonight and the nearest government clinic. Why: location makes a map useful in the first week. How for MESH: browser geolocation, computed on the device, never sent. `function | M | low | Nomad List, Settlement.org`

## G. Language: glossary and phrasebook

- **G1. Glossary of local terms.** Iqama, muqeem, kafeel, jawazat, istiqdam, ta'jeel, i'tithaar, mukafaa, sakan, mustawa, zaer, hawiya, Absher, Nafath, Ejar, Kulliyah, Ma'had: Arabic script, transliteration, plain meaning, the guide where it matters. Why: Settlement.org and Expatica glossaries cut confusion; search already has these as synonyms. How for MESH: `/glossary` page from one data file, also fed into search. `content | S | high | Settlement.org, Expatica`
- **G2. Auto-linked terms in guides.** The first use of a glossary term in a guide is underlined; tap shows the meaning. Why: readers stop at unknown words; an inline popover keeps them on the page. How for MESH: a remark plugin over guide markdown using the glossary file. `UX | M | medium | Wikipedia previews, GOV.UK`
- **G3. Situational phrasebook.** Taxi, pharmacy, landlord, University office, barber, tailor, doctor: Hijazi everyday phrases and the Fusha form, with audio. Why: Wikivoyage "Talk" and Lonely Planet phrasebooks are among their most used parts. How for MESH: a page per situation, short audio recorded by a volunteer, tie-in to Ma'had vocabulary. `content | M | medium | Wikivoyage Talk, Lonely Planet`
- **G4. Show-the-clerk cards.** Full-screen Arabic sentences: "I need an enrolment letter for the bank", "My iqama has not arrived", "Where is the women's section?". Why: travel phrase cards beat speaking when stressed. How for MESH: large type cards, offline, one tap from the relevant guide step. `UX | S | medium | travel phrase cards`

## H. Safety, health and emergencies

- **H1. Emergency page that works offline.** Unified and specific emergency numbers, MOH 937, the University emergency line, what to say in Arabic, how to share your location. Why: the roadmap has "important numbers"; the phrasing and location help are what make it usable in a crisis. How for MESH: precached page, numbers confirmed before publishing, call links. `content | S | high | Wikivoyage Stay safe, GOV.UK travel advice`
- **H2. Embassy and consulate list by nationality.** UK, US, Canada, Ireland, Australia, Nigeria, South Africa and others, with how to register with your embassy for alerts. Why: GOV.UK travel advice tells nationals to sign up for updates; in a regional emergency this matters. How for MESH: a table linked from H1 and from the onboarding nationality answer. `content | S | medium | GOV.UK travel advice`
- **H3. Health system navigator.** Insurance card, the student clinic, booking through Sehhaty, 937, which care needs which place, women's clinics. Why: Settlement.org and Melbourne explain the health system before people are ill. How for MESH: extends the healthcare guide with a "where to go for what" table. `content | M | high | Settlement.org, Melbourne OSHC`
- **H4. Lost or stolen documents.** Lost passport, lost iqama (report through Absher), lost student card: steps, offices, fees. Why: Citizens Advice and GOV.UK "lost passport abroad" pages are high traffic. How for MESH: a short guide linked from H1. `content | S | medium | GOV.UK, Citizens Advice`
- **H5. Settling in well.** Homesickness, culture shock stages, first-semester fatigue, family strain, the University counselling service, with an Islamic framing of patience and companionship. Why: every international office has a culture shock page. How for MESH: a guide written with authority, no rulings, ending with "message Admin on WhatsApp". `content | S | medium | Melbourne, U of T, NUS`

## I. Trust and freshness signals

- **I1. Public "What changed" page.** `/changes`: dated one-line entries for material changes (fees, visa rules, new guides, corrected steps), filterable by topic. Why: GOV.UK travel advice keeps a full change history; it is the strongest trust signal for rules that move. How for MESH: generated from guide `latestUpdate` fields and admin updates; feed and push. `trust | M | high | GOV.UK change history`
- **I2. "Latest update" line on each guide.** One sentence under the title: "Exit re-entry fees updated for 1448". Why: GOV.UK shows the latest change above the content so readers know if they need to re-read. How for MESH: one frontmatter field, shown with the last-checked date. `trust | S | high | GOV.UK travel advice`
- **I3. Follow a guide.** "Tell me when this guide changes" by push or email. Why: GOV.UK "get emails about this page". How for MESH: reuse the push follow system built for shaykhs, keyed by guide slug. `feature | M | medium | GOV.UK`
- **I4. Freshness colours for last checked.** Green under 3 months, gold under 6, grey older; the admin app lists grey guides first. Why: Numbeo and Nomad List show data dates; MESH already stores last-verified dates. How for MESH: badge component plus an admin "due for re-check" list (roadmap item 11). `trust | S | medium | Numbeo, Nomad List`
- **I5. Rules updates as their own category.** Official changes (fees, visa, iqama, University rules) in a separate stream from duroos changes, pinned on path pages. Why: expat sites keep "news" apart from guides so rule changes are not lost. How for MESH: a category on the existing updates model. `content | S | medium | Expatica news, GOV.UK`
- **I6. "Was this page useful?" and "Something wrong?"** One tap opens WhatsApp to Admin with the page title and link filled in. Why: GOV.UK's feedback footer is how they find stale pages. How for MESH: footer component; analytics counts taps without storing IPs. `trust | S | high | GOV.UK feedback`

## J. Community, kept moderated and impartial

- **J1. Ask a question, get an answered FAQ.** A form (or prefilled WhatsApp) sends a question to the admin queue; the answer is published as an FAQ, never as an open thread. Why: Expat.com checks every post; Reddit wikis grow from repeated questions; MESH keeps quality and impartiality. How for MESH: admin "Questions" inbox that turns into an FAQ draft with one tap. `community | M | high | Expat.com, Reddit wikis, Settlement.org forum`
- **J2. Most asked this month.** A public panel of the questions most searched or asked this month, each linked to its answer. Why: social proof and fast routing; Reddit pins its weekly megathread. How for MESH: from search logs (no IPs) and the FAQ queue. `community | S | medium | Reddit wikis`
- **J3. Welcome buddy.** New arrivals can ask Admin to be paired with a senior student of the same path (brother with brother, sister with sister). Why: InterNations ambassadors welcome new members; universities run peer mentors. How for MESH: a WhatsApp prefilled request; matching done by admins; no public profiles. `community | M | high | InterNations ambassadors, uni peer mentors`
- **J4. New arrivals gatherings.** Each semester, separate brothers' and sisters' welcome gatherings listed on the site with a calendar file. Why: InterNations and orientation weeks show that the first meetup decides whether people settle. How for MESH: the planned events feature with a "new arrivals" tag. `community | S | medium | InterNations events, orientation weeks`
- **J5. "How I did it" notes.** Short first-person accounts (family visa, childbirth, renting) edited by MESH and attached to the matching guide. Why: Expat.com and InterNations' Expat Insider show that real experience is what readers trust. How for MESH: an "experiences" block on guides; names optional, edited for tone and impartiality. `content | M | medium | Expat.com, InterNations Expat Insider`
- **J6. Leaving sale board, admin run.** Students leaving post furniture and books through Admin; listings expire after 30 days. Why: Expat.com classifieds are heavily used; leaving students and arriving students meet in the same month. How for MESH: low priority; only individuals, no businesses, auto-expiry. `community | M | low | Expat.com classifieds`

## K. Offline, print and share

- **K1. Printable arrival pack.** One PDF or print view: the timeline, documents to carry, numbers, glossary, route cards, show-the-clerk cards. Why: Settlement.org's "First Days" PDF and Wikivoyage printouts are what people carry. How for MESH: a print stylesheet on `/start/print`, generated from live content so it never drifts. `content | M | high | Settlement.org First Days PDF, Wikivoyage`
- **K2. Save a guide for offline.** A button that caches the guide, its images and the glossary in the service worker. Why: offline travel apps (Lonely Planet, Wikivoyage offline) are used when roaming data is off, which is exactly the arrival week. How for MESH: Cache API keyed by guide slug; "Saved" list on the dashboard. `performance | M | high | Lonely Planet app, Wikivoyage offline`
- **K3. Send this step to someone.** Share one checklist step or journey to WhatsApp with a deep link to that step. Why: families plan together; a husband sends the visa step to his wife. How for MESH: anchor links on steps plus the planned share cards. `UX | S | medium | GOV.UK step links`
- **K4. QR posters for orientation.** QR codes to the path pages for the Ma'had and Kulliyah welcome days and the sakan noticeboards. Why: corporate relocation teams hand out one link on day one. How for MESH: printable poster page in MESH colours. `operations | S | medium | company relocation guides`

## M. Leaving and moving on

- **M1. Leaving Madinah journey.** Final exit or exit re-entry, University clearance, dorm hand-back, closing the bank, ending Ejar, shipping books, attesting graduation documents.
